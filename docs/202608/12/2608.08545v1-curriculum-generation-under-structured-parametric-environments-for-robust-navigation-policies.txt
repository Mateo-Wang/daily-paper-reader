# Curriculum Generation under Structured Parametric Environments for Robust Navigation Policies 

Prishita Ray ∗

Cornell University pr376@cornell.edu 

Abstract: Robust navigation policies for autonomous agents must generalize across continuously varying environmental conditions such as turn rates, obsta-cles, friction, pits, and slopes. Curriculum generation provides a principled mech-anism for improving generalization by progressively adapting training environ-ments, but designing such curricula in a sample-efficient and automated manner remains challenging. This paper proposes a reparameterized curriculum gener-ation framework for structured continuous environment parameters using unidi-rectional gradient-based optimization. To improve robustness in multimodal ob-servation spaces consisting of image-based and scalar inputs, a distribution-shift regularization objective is incorporated to encourage the learning of finer-grained latent representations. The proposed method is evaluated across two continuous-control OpenAI Gym environments: a 2D obstacle-based Car Racing variant and Bipedal Walker variant, where coupled environment parameters jointly influence policy performance. Across five random seeds, our method consistently out-performs vanilla policy training, random parameter sampling, manual curricula, frontier-based methods, Self-Paced Reinforcement Learning (SPRL), Absolute Learning Progress with Gaussian Mixture Models (ALP-GMM), and reverse cur-riculum learning baselines. Ablation studies further demonstrate the effectiveness of the reparameterized curriculum mechanism across both environments, while highlighting environment-dependent benefits of the auxiliary regularization ob-jective. 

Keywords: Curriculum Generation, Continuous Control, Robust Navigation 

## 1 Introduction 

Model-free reinforcement learning (RL) is widely used for learning navigation and locomotion poli-cies in simulated robotic environments. However, training on fixed environment distributions often limits robustness to deployment variation. Curriculum learning addresses this challenge by adap-tively selecting training environments [1, 2]. Existing curriculum methods commonly rely on black-box optimization [3] or adversarial/minimax formulations [4, 5], which can be computationally expensive or unstable in continuous parameterized environments. This paper proposes a reparameterized curriculum generation framework for low-dimensional con-tinuous environment parameter spaces. Our contributions are: • A gradient-based curriculum adaptation method over coupled continuous environment pa-rameters. • A distribution-shift regularization objective for more stable curriculum evolution in image-based settings. • Evaluation on modified Car Racing and Bipedal Walker environments with continuously varying difficulty parameters.

## 2 Related Work 

Curriculum learning and adaptive environment generation have been widely studied for improving robustness and generalization in RL [6, 7, 8, 9]. 

Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) methods generate adversarial or progressively difficult tasks using teacher policies or environment generators [10, 11, 12, 13, 4, 14, 5]. These methods have been applied across navigation, procedural generation, and robotics domains [15, 16, 17, 18]. While effective, many rely on black-box or population-based optimization that can become computation-ally expensive in continuous parameterized environments. 

Robust and minimax RL approaches formulate training as a game against adversarial perturbations [19, 20, 21]. Bayesian optimization has also been explored for curriculum adaptation [3], though such approaches remain computationally intensive and non-differentiable. 

Automatic curriculum learning methods such as SPRL [22], CURROT [23], ALP-GMM [24], PLR [25] and ACuTE [26] adapt continuously parameterized environments using competence- or progress-based sampling strategies. However, these methods rely on stochastic task selection rather than direct gradient-based updates over coupled environment parameters. 

Domain randomization improves robustness by sampling diverse environment parameters [27], but static sampling does not adapt to learner competence. Our method performs gradient-based curriculum adaptation directly over continuous environment parameter distributions, enabling efficient and controlled curriculum evolution in structured para-metric environments.

## 4 Methodology 

4.1 Reparameterization Curriculum 

Two modules are used in the Reparameterization Curriculum (Reparam): the policy and the critic (see Fig. 2 and Algorithm 1). The policy network parameterized by θ learns control policies condi-tioned on environment observations x and environment parameters ψ, while the critic uses the value function network parameterized by ϕ adapts curriculum difficulty through gradient-based updates over continuous environment parameter bounds. For Car Racing, the policy uses a multi-input architecture where RGB observations are processed using a CNN and environment parameters ψ = ( κ, p ) are encoded using an MLP before feature fusion. For Bipedal Walker, proprioceptive states are concatenated with ψ = ( f, ρ, s ) and processed using an MLP. PPO is used to optimize the policy using clipped policy and value objectives.

Reparam is compared against the following baselines on the test environments Etest :

Vanilla Policy: Training is performed only on the default minimum-difficulty environment param-eters ψmin without curriculum adaptation. 

Random Sampling: Training environments are uniformly sampled from the full parameter range 

ψi ∼ U [ψmin , ψ max ] without curriculum growth. 

Manual Curriculum: The curriculum upper bounds ψt increase linearly from ψmin to ψmax 

throughout training. 

SPRL: Self-Paced Reinforcement Learning (SPRL) [22] maintains a Gaussian curriculum distribu-tion ψi ∼ N (μt, Σt) where the distribution parameters (μt, Σt) are adapted using policy compe-tence estimates and progressively shifted toward more difficult environments. 

ALP-GMM: Absolute Learning Progress with Gaussian Mixture Models (ALP-GMM) [24] main-tains a Gaussian mixture curriculum distribution ψi ∼ PKk=1 wkN (μk, Σk) where environments are sampled from regions associated with high absolute learning progress. 

Frontier Curriculum: A gradient-based curriculum variant where environments are sampled only from a local frontier region ψi ∈ (ψt − ∆, ψ t) instead of the full running curriculum range ψi ∈

## 6 Conclusion 

In this paper, a sample-efficient curriculum generation method is presented based on reparameter-ization and auxiliary distribution-shift regularization to train robust RL navigation policies in con-tinuous environment parameter spaces. It demonstrated competitive performance against adaptive curriculum baselines while balancing metrics across varying environment conditions. ## A Policy-Critic Architecture 

Figure 5 illustrates the architectures of the policy and critic networks for the Car Racing and Bipedal Walker environments. Before policy and curriculum optimization, raw environment observations are processed into appropriate feature representations. For Car Racing, a multimodal Nature CNN + MLP network is employed: 96 × 96 × 3 RGB track images x are processed through a CNN, while the 2-element environment parameters ψ = ( κ, p ) are encoded via an MLP; the resulting features are then fused for policy learning. For Bipedal Walker, 24-element proprioceptive state vectors x are concatenated with 3-element environment vectors ψ = ( f, ρ, s ) and processed through an MLP. The policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with clipped policy and value objectives. Additionally, the value network is leveraged to compute gradients with respect to the running curriculum bounds ψt, which are used to adaptively adjust environment difficulty.
