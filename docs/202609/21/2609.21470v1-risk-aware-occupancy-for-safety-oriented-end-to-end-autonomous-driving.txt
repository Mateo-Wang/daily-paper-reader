Abstract 

Sparse representation formulates the environment perception for the end-to-end driving system as a set of discrete elements like objects and lane lines. This formulation meets safety risks in crowded, occluded scenes dealing with unstructured ob-stacles, uncertain regions, and intricate interactions. In this paper, we propose a dense representation, risk-aware occu-pancy, to characterize planning-relevant risks in an explicit and uniform manner. It jointly encodes global scene occu-pancy, map-derived traffic constraints, and future dynamic agent occupancy into a unified BEV map. The unified BEV map captures the risk evidence for trajectory planning in both spatial and temporal dimensions. We design an E2E network, ROIDrive, to realize risk-aware occupancy. It predicts risk-aware occupancy with an independent branch and injects it into planning queries for safety-oriented trajectory genera-tion. In addition, to quantify the safety problem, we intro-duce RiskOcc4D-nuScenes built upon nuscenes and occ3d-nuscenes. Our risk-aware occupancy yields relative open-loop collision reductions of 52.9% under the UniAD metric and 

35.0% under the ST-P3 metric on nuScenes. 

## Introduction 

Safety is one of the core problems blocking the wide applica-tion of both L2 and L4 autonomous driving. Current end-to-end methods have made great progress on the safety problem, but the sparse representation makes it hard to completely and explicitly tackle the planning-relevant risks. When the envi-ronment is encoded into discrete agents and maps elements, a natural safety risk arises in dealing with unstructured ele-ments and occluded regions. In contrast, a dense represen-tation could describe the scene as a continuous spatial field. Then the planner can explicitly check whether candidate tra-jectories are feasible, and measure the conflict risk around them. In this paper, we propose risk-aware occupancy as the dense representation and make it flexible to help existing e2e frameworks become safety-oriented. By diving into the detailed scenes of safe driving, we sum-marize two key attributes for the dense representation. As il-lustrated in Figure 1, first, it should continuously distinguish drivable from non-drivable space, which has an arbitrary shape, in the time dimension. Then, it should evaluate spa-tial conflicts by predicting how these conflicts evolve over time. These insights follow the intuitive human driving prin-ciples, where a driver will determine whether the surround

It aligns the perception objective with downstream decisions and provides a direct interface for safety inspection, failure localization, and visual debugging. Besides, to ensure effi-cient performance, we also tokenize the dense risk field by sliding-window sampling and inject it into the agent queries via cross-attention, staying orthogonal to the sparse tokens. ## Related Work 

Occupancy Prediction Methods 

Recently, occupancy prediction has advanced rapidly along three axes. Lightweight architectures : FlashOcc (Yu et al. 2023), TPVFormer (Huang et al. 2023), SparseOcc (Tang et al. 2024), FastOcc (Hou et al. 2024), and Octree-Occ (Lu et al. 2023) reduce cost via BEV extraction, tri-plane decom-position, sparse encoding, and octree reconstruction, respec-tively. Performance optimization : FB-Occ (Li et al. 2023b) fuses forward-backward projection (Huang et al. 2021; Li et al. 2022) with multi-task learning; F-OCC (Zhao, Zheng, and Gong 2024) extends DCNv4 (Xiong et al. 2024) to 3D; OccTransformer (Liu et al. 2024), OccFormer (Zhang, Zhu, and Du 2023), VoxelFormer (Li et al. 2023a), and MambaOcc (Tian et al. 2024) leverage advanced atten-tion or state-space architectures. Cross-domain integration :NeRF-based methods (Pan et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2024b; Mildenhall et al. 2021), Gaussian splat-ting variants (Huang et al. 2024c,a; Gan et al. 2024; Kerbl et al. 2023), world models (Zheng et al. 2025), and LLM-driven approaches (Xu et al. 2025; Wei et al. 2024) further enrich occupancy representations from complementary per-spectives.

## Risk-Aware Occupancy Perception 

Conventional 3D semantic occupancy builds high-entropy voxel representations H(V) with fine-grained categories; though complete, they carry substantial task-irrelevant re-dundancy Hred (V) that degrades risk-information transfer for E2E driving. Risk-aware occupancy prediction is formulated as planning-oriented minimal sufficient statistic extraction: 

R∗ = arg max 

R I(R; Tplan ), s.t. dim( R) ≪ dim( V),

(1) where I(R; Tplan ) is the mutual information between risk representation R and the optimal trajectory Tplan .Moreover, conventional 3D occupancy is over-complete: its semantic paradigm cannot uniformly quantify collision threats and regulatory constraints ( e.g. , lane markings, stop lines). Risk-aware occupancy encodes both risk types and resolves conflicts via maximum aggregation over collision and regulatory terms. To realize this formulation, dual-axis reduction is performed: 

• Spatial: Only the critical height interval Hcrit ∈

[0 m , 2.2 m] (the vehicle-relevant collision zone) is re-tained, discarding non-threatening airspace. 

• Semantic: Fine-grained labels are replaced by object height as the primary risk quantifier; vulnerable road users (VRUs) retain semantic priors with elevated risk weights. A hierarchical risk quantification framework is con-structed over the Occ3D-nuScenes perception domain Ω = 

{(x, y ) | x, y ∈ [−40 m , 40 m] }. Critical heights are non-linearly mapped to a normalized risk score Znorm ∈ [0 , 1] . To separate risk modes, static global obstacle risk is bounded to V global risk ∈ [0 .2, 0.7] and moving objects to a dedicated branch with V dyn risk ∈ [0 .5, 1.0] . Their deliberate overlap en-forces a strict priority hierarchy—dynamic collision > static obstacle > regulatory risk—so dynamic threats receive plan-ning priority while gradient conflict in multi-task optimiza-tion is mitigated. 

ROIDrive Framework 

Design rationale. As illustrated in Figure 2, ROIDrive builds on the query-based generative E2E paradigm (Zheng et al. 2024; Jiang et al. 2023), where a BEVFormer backbone yields a BEV feature B ∈ RC×H×W , a small fixed set of agent and map queries abstracts the scene, and a conditional VAE planner decodes multimodal trajectories. This compres-sion causes two structural weaknesses: representation spar-sity , as the dense, temporally evolving risk structure of the BEV plane is left implicit, and token sparsity and unreliabil-ity , as missed detections erase risk while false-positive and flickering queries inject spurious motion intent. ROIDrive thus pursues two goals: (1) to introduce a perception modal-ity that is independently predicted and then fused into the E2E pipeline, and (2) to densify the representation and cal-ibrate token reliability. Both are met by two modules at the motion-prediction stage: a risk-aware occupancy (RiskOcc) branch and a Temporal Query Consistency (TQC) module. 

Risk-aware occupancy branch. The BEV feature is channel-reduced, modeled by a Bi-Mamba2 state-space block that captures long-range dependencies at O(N ) cost, and upsampled. A lookup table converts the stable class pre-dictions to a normalized risk field R ∈ [0 , 0.7] ; after optional fusion with an SDMap+ prior, R is tokenized by sliding-window sampling and injected into the queries via cross-attention. The field is dense and orthogonal to the queries: the queries encode object identity and location, while the field encodes the current and near-future risk at each lo-cation. It also provides a detection-free fallback for missed hazards, since R is regressed directly from B, and anticipates risk through long-range scanning and future supervision.

## RiskOcc4D-nuScenes Dataset 

Conventional 3D occupancy datasets suffer from spatial re-dundancy, semantics misaligned with E2E planning, the ab-sence of a unified collision-regulatory risk encoding, and missing temporal risk annotations. We address these limi-tations with RiskOcc4D-nuScenes , built upon nuScenes and Occ3D-nuScenes—the first 4D risk-aware occupancy bench-mark for E2E autonomous driving. Four automated pipelines unify static global risk, traffic regulatory risk, and dynamic future risk into a single framework while remaining fully compatible with existing 3D occupancy algorithms. 

Task Definition 

Combining the avoidance-priority principle with Bayesian risk decision theory, we instantiate the abstract optimal rep-resentation R∗ in Eq. (1) by minimizing the bottleneck risk that aligns perception with planning: 

L(Tplan | Z) = E[max( ℓcol (Tplan , O ), ℓ reg (Tplan , M )) | Z] ,

(2) where Z is the multi-view input, (ℓcol , O ) the collision risk and obstacle set, and (ℓreg , M ) the regulatory risk and traffic-rule constraints. The max operator enforces hard-constraint alignment, avoiding accumulation overflow and inter-term gradient conflict, thereby providing the supervisory signal for the risk-aware occupancy maps in Section .

Output. Ego-centric BEV risk-aware occupancy maps: a global map RM t ∈ RH×W , where occupancy encodes spa-tial location and risk values quantify hazard attention carry-ing collision and regulatory information, and a dynamic map

## Experiments 

We evaluate multiple 3D occupancy backbones on RiskOcc4D-nuScenes and its downstream utility for E2E driving. 

Experimental Setup 

Implementation details All occupancy baselines use their default configurations with only the output heads replaced: the classification head retains each method’s original super-vision, while both regression heads use the Huber loss (Huber 1964). Training runs on multiple NVIDIA A100 GPUs; in-ference and benchmarking use an Intel i9-13900K CPU with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

End-to-End Autonomous Driving Integration 

Experimental Design We integrate the risk-aware occu-pancy map into GenAD (Zheng et al. 2024), an E2E plan-ner encoding dynamic agents and road topology as instance and map tokens. Such discrete object-level representations are spatially incomplete, capturing only detected targets while lacking explicit encoding of dense spatial risk, long-tail obstacles, and ambiguous occupied regions—gaps that our dense, continuous risk distribution complements. Let 

Zinst+map and Zrisk denote the joint instance-and-map and the risk representations. Our central hypothesis is a positive conditional mutual information gain: 

∆I = I(Asafe ; Zrisk | Z inst+map ) > 0, (4) i.e., the risk representation carries safety-relevant informa-tion not captured by instance and map tokens. The risk map is sampled via a sliding window and fused with GenAD’s instance tokens through cross-attention (Figure 4), allowing each instance token to attend to surrounding grid-level risk. GenAD’s original training and evaluation protocols remain unchanged and the risk map enters solely as an additional input, ensuring all gains are attributable to the risk represen-tation. Comparative Results Table 2 reports collision rates on the nuScenes validation set. Ours substantially reduces collisions over GenAD under both metrics: ↓52.9% on UniAD (Hu et al. 2023) (0.17% → 0.08% Avg.) and ↓35.0% on ST-P3 (Hu et al. 2022) (0.40% → 0.26% Avg.), consistently across all horizons and largest at the 2 s horizon (UniAD ↓73.3%; ST-P3 ↓51.7%), where accumulated trajectory uncertainty is high and dense spatial risk priors are most valuable. Ablation Study Each ablation replaces the RiskOcc Map with an alternative spatial representation while keeping the fusion module and all other settings fixed (Table 3). 

3D-Occ. Conventional 3D semantic voxels are adap-tively pooled to the RiskOcc Map size and fused identically. Gains are mixed—slightly better than baseline under ST-P3 (0.37 vs. 0.40) but worse under UniAD (0.20 vs. 0.17): al-though raw voxels carry high mutual information, adaptive pooling lowers the effective information rate and planning-irrelevant semantic redundancy adds noise. By distilling safety-critical information at construction time, RiskOcc at-tains higher task-relevant density in a lower-dimensional BEV, with consistent gains under both protocols (0.26 / 0.08). 

Binary Map. Collapsing continuous risk to a single bit (risk = 1, no risk = 0) erases ordinal relationships among threat levels. The resulting rates (ST-P3 Avg. 0.48; UniAD Avg. 0.24) are far worse than RiskOcc (0.26 / 0.08) and even degrade over the no-map baseline (0.40 / 0.17), showing that a gradient-free binary signal misleads the planner. Hence risk stratification, rather than mere spatial occupancy, constitutes the core mechanism behind the safety gains. 

Gaussian Map. As a control, a random Gaussian map of identical size but carrying no risk semantics yields the worst rates across all metrics (ST-P3 Avg. 0.55; UniAD Avg. 0.33), below the no-map baseline, ruling out gains from the added cross-attention parameters and confirming that RiskOcc’s benefit stems from semantically meaningful risk encoding. Together, these ablations rule out network capacity, high-dimensional semantics, and binary occupancy as alternative explanations, providing evidence consistent with—though not a direct measurement of—the conditional mutual infor-mation gain ∆I > 0 in Eq. (4).

## Conclusion 

This paper introduced risk-aware occupancy as a new per-ception representation for E2E autonomous driving. We constructed RiskOcc4D-nuScenes, fully compatible with Occ3D-nuScenes, under a standardized multi-level eval-uation protocol. We validated our 3D occupancy meth-ods and demonstrated their compatibility and transferabil-ity. By fusing the predicted risk-aware occupancy map into GenAD (Zheng et al. 2024) via sliding-window sampling, we yielded collision-rate reductions of 52.9% (UniAD) and 35.0% (ST-P3). We further designed the ROIDrive, an E2E framework coupling an independent risk-aware occupancy branch with temporal query consistency for safety-oriented planning.
