Abstract 

Counterfactual world models (CWM) extract motion from pretrained video predictors by comparing factual and intervened predictions. However, responses generated under different target-frame masks vary in reliability, while uniform aggregation weights them equally. We formulate response aggregation as candidate reliability learning and propose LPA-CWM with a lightweight Learned Physical Adjudicator (LPA). Trained on dense MOVi-F trajectories, the 3.0M-parameter LPA compares visual context and response structure across an unordered candidate set to predict relative weights, while the CWM predictor and intervention generator remain frozen. The weighted responses undergo windowed localization and one paired re-evaluation to recover motion. We also introduce Completeness-aware Motion Correspondence (CMC), a ground-truth-anchored evaluation protocol that jointly measures localization, trajec-tory completeness, visibility, and continuity, counting missing predictions as failures on visible dynamic points. On the evaluated DAVIS and Kinetics subsets, LPA-CWM improves DCA avg

1arXiv:2609.14073v1 [cs.CV] 12 Sep 2026 1 Introduction 

World models learn predictive representations of visual dynamics (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2019; 2020). Counterfactual World Modeling (CWM) extracts motion from these representa-tions by comparing predictions with and without localized interventions (Bear et al., 2023; Venkatesh et al., 2024; Stojanov et al., 2025). Different target-frame masks can produce inconsistent responses for the same query: some localize the motion well, while others are affected by background content, competing motion, or visual ambiguity. Uniform aggregation treats all responses as equally reliable and can dilute useful evidence. This raises two questions: how should counterfactual motion evidence be combined, and how should the recovered trajectories be evaluated? To improve CWM inference, we formulate multi-mask aggregation as candidate reliability estima-tion. We first derive APA-CWM, an Analytic Physical Adjudicator (APA) from the Normalized Spatiotemporal Gradient (NSG) (Zhang et al., 2025). APA reads the NSG-derived spatiotemporal consistency around each candidate response peak and converts these local scores into closed-form reliability weights. It provides a training-free physical reference for testing how far an analytic consistency prior can resolve candidate ambiguity. We then propose LPA-CWM, whose Learned Physical Adjudicator (LPA) combines local visual evidence, response morphology, shared frame-pair context, and candidate statistics. A Transformer without candidate-index positional embeddings jointly compares the unordered candidate set and predicts relative weights. We train only the 3.0M-parameter LPA on dense Kubric MOVi-F trajectories (Greff et al., 2022). For each query, dense forward motion provides the oracle target endpoint; candidate endpoint errors define relative reliability targets and supervise distribution matching, weighted-coordinate localization, and best-candidate ranking. This gives direct supervision for which CWM responses are trustworthy while leaving the pretrained CWM unchanged. At inference, LPA weights the initial responses, followed by windowed localization and one paired CWM re-evaluation to obtain the final motion. Existing benchmarks assess video quality, temporal consistency, physical and commonsense faith-fulness, controllability, embodied utility, and counterfactual consistency (Huang et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2025; Duan et al., 2025; Shang et al., 2026; Begiristain et al., 2026). They broaden evaluation beyond visual plausibility, but do not directly measure point correspondence recovered through CWM interventions. In motion recovery, low error on returned predictions can hide missing trajectory segments, while smooth trajectories may still follow incorrect motion. We therefore introduce Completeness-aware Motion Correspondence (CMC) , a ground-truth-anchored protocol built on TAP-Vid annotations (Doersch et al., 2022). CMC evaluates localization, completeness, visibility, and temporal continuity on dynamic point tracks. Dynamic Correspondence Accuracy requires visible predictions within a distance threshold and counts missing predictions as failures on ground-truth-visible points. Track-level success further requires sufficient coverage and bounded localization error, while visibility and continuity diagnostics expose interrupted trajectories. CMC complements TAP-Vid First by making completeness on moving tracks an explicit target. We evaluate on DAVIS and Kinetics (Pont-Tuset et al., 2017; Kay et al., 2017) against SMURF, GMRW, DODUO, CoTracker3, RAFT, and SEA-RAFT, covering self-supervised flow, global corre-spondence, dense optical flow, and dedicated point tracking (Stone et al., 2021; Shrivastava & Owens, 2024; Jiang et al., 2024; Karaev et al., 2025; Teed & Deng, 2020; Wang et al., 2024). LPA-CWM improves DCA avg over Uniform CWM by 60.0% on DAVIS and 29.0% on Kinetics. On Kinetics it achieves the best value across all reported CMC metrics; on DAVIS it gives the highest coverage, Complete@0.8, and continuity, while CoTracker3 remains stronger on several conventional tracking measures. Within the CWM family, LPA-CWM also improves all TAP-Vid First measures over Uniform CWM and APA-CWM. In summary, our main contributions are: • We formulate multi-mask CWM inference as candidate reliability estimation and introduce analytic and learned reliability weighting for counterfactual motion responses. • We propose LPA-CWM, a lightweight physically supervised adjudicator trained from dense MOVi-F trajectories, with windowed localization and one paired CWM re-evaluation. • We introduce CMC for joint evaluation of localization, completeness, visibility, and continu-ity, and show strong gains over Uniform CWM, APA-CWM, and external baselines. 22 Related Work 

World models and counterfactual motion. Video world models learn predictive representations through feature prediction, as in V-JEPA (Bardes et al., 2024), or action-controllable generation, as in Genie (Bruce et al., 2024). CWM extracts correspondence by comparing factual and lo-cally intervened predictions (Bear et al., 2023), and later work applies this interface to physical dynamics (Venkatesh et al., 2024). Opt-CWM learns query-conditioned perturbations for flow and oc-clusion (Stojanov et al., 2025); KL-tracing measures the response of generative video models through KL divergence (Kim et al., 2025); and Point Prompting propagates colored markers through video diffusion models (Shrivastava et al., 2026). LPA-CWM focuses on learning the relative reliability of the multiple responses already produced by a frozen CWM. 

Diffusion models as structural priors. Diffusion features also support visual correspondence. SD4Match adapts Stable Diffusion features with image-pair-conditioned prompts (Li et al., 2024), while Track4Gen jointly trains video generation and point correspondence to reduce appearance drift (Jeong et al., 2025). NSG relates a diffusion score field to observed inter-frame changes as a spatiotemporal consistency signal (Zhang et al., 2025). APA-CWM uses this signal for analytic candidate weighting, while LPA-CWM learns the weights from trajectory supervision. 

Flow, correspondence, and point tracking. Our external baselines cover complementary mo-tion interfaces. RAFT uses all-pairs correlations with iterative flow refinement (Teed & Deng, 2020), and SEA-RAFT improves the framework with probabilistic training and a more efficient design (Wang et al., 2024). SMURF learns optical flow through full-image warping and multi-frame self-supervision (Stone et al., 2021). GMRW learns global correspondence with contrastive random walks (Shrivastava & Owens, 2024), whereas DODUO combines semantic priors with self-supervised flow (Jiang et al., 2024). CoTracker3 is a dedicated point tracker trained with pseudo-labels from real videos (Karaev et al., 2025); AllTracker extends dense long-range tracking with multi-frame flow and temporal attention (Harley et al., 2025). 

Evaluation of motion consistency. General video and world-model benchmarks measure comple-mentary properties: VBench targets visual quality and temporal consistency (Huang et al., 2024), VBench-2.0 adds physical and commonsense faithfulness (Zheng et al., 2025), WorldScore evalu-ates controllability, quality, and dynamics (Duan et al., 2025), WorldArena includes embodied task utility (Shang et al., 2026), and CRONOS tests counterfactual physical consistency (Begiristain et al., 2026). Motion-specific evaluation includes FVMD for point-track motion distributions (Liu et al., 2024), Physics-IQ for prediction against real physical experiments (Motamed et al., 2026), and TAPVid-3D for long-range 3D correspondence and visibility (Koppula et al., 2024). CMC instead evaluates recovered 2D dynamic tracks, counting missing visible predictions as failures and reporting completeness and continuity alongside localization. 

3 Method 

Given two consecutive frames and a query point, our method first uses a frozen counterfactual world model (CWM) to produce multiple motion candidates. We then compare two ways to weight these candidates: an analytic rule, APA-CWM, and a learned adjudicator, LPA-CWM. Both variants combine the candidate response maps before applying the same localization and re-evaluation procedure. Figure 2 summarizes the method.

3.2 Analytic Candidate Weighting 

APA-CWM provides a closed-form reference based on the Normalized Spatiotemporal Gradient (NSG) (Zhang et al., 2025). Let xt, xt+1 ∈ [−1, 1] 224 ×224 ×3 denote the frame pair (It, It+1 ) after preprocessing for a frozen Ablated Diffusion Model (ADM) (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021). The notation distinguishes these ADM inputs from the 256 × 256 RGB frames used by CWM. Let sϕ be the frozen ADM score network, ts its diffusion time, and u a location on the ADM grid. Following NSG, we compute a spatial score P that compares the local ADM score magnitude with its alignment to the observed frame change:

3.3 Learned Physical Adjudicator 

Candidate representation and set reasoning. LPA represents each candidate using four sources of information. (1) Local visual evidence: the local branch receives a 10-channel 32 × 32 tensor containing source and target RGB crops, their absolute RGB difference, and a crop of the candidate response. These inputs correspond to 64 × 64 regions centered at pt and bp(m)

> t+1

(2) Full response: a second branch encodes the complete edm map to retain the global shape and ambiguity of the response. (3) Frame context: a third branch encodes the full frame pair to provide scene-level visual information shared by all candidates. (4) Candidate statistics: the final branch encodes 16 scalar descriptors covering displacement, response concentration, ambiguity, photometric change, and edge strength. The exact descriptors and encoder architectures are given in Appendix A. The four encoded features are combined and projected to a 256-dimensional candidate token zm. 4 Experiments 

Our experiments mainly address three questions: (1) Does learned candidate weighting improve motion recovered from a frozen CWM? (2) How does LPA-CWM compare with analytic weighting and external motion baselines under CMC and standard point-tracking evaluation? (3) Which components and weighting behaviors contribute to the improvement? 

4.1 Experimental Setup 

Models and data. Uniform CWM, APA-CWM, and LPA-CWM share the same frozen predictor, intervention generator, and cached initial responses. They differ only in candidate weighting and use the same localization and paired re-evaluation procedure. We fix M = 10 , use one paired re-evaluation ( R = 1 ), set βπ = 200 , and apply two windowed localization updates. Only the 3.0M-parameter LPA is trained on MOVi-F trajectories, with videos split before query sampling and checkpoint selection performed on held-out MOVi-F videos. We evaluate on 30 videos each from DAVIS (Pont-Tuset et al., 2017) and Kinetics (Kay et al., 2017), using TAP-Vid point tracks and visibility annotations (Doersch et al., 2022). The same evaluation videos and queries are used for all methods; further protocol details are provided in Appendix B. 4.3 TAP-Vid First and Qualitative Results 

TAP-Vid First is a standard evaluation protocol from TAP-Vid that queries each point at its first visible frame and evaluates the subsequent trajectory (Doersch et al., 2022). Within the CWM family, LPA-CWM achieves the best result on all five reported measures on both DAVIS and Kinetics (Table 2), consistently improving over Uniform CWM and APA-CWM. External trackers provide an additional reference under this conventional tracking protocol; CoTracker3 remains stronger on several measures, particularly on DAVIS. Together with the CMC results, these comparisons show that learned candidate weighting improves both conventional tracking accuracy and the completeness of motion recovered from the frozen CWM. Figure 5 provides four qualitative comparisons, including a manipulation case together with human-action and animal-motion scenes. LPA-CWM gives the lowest displayed mean EPE in each example and remains stable under large motion and appearance changes. 5 Conclusion and Limitations 

We presented LPA-CWM for improving motion reasoning with a frozen counterfactual world model. LPA learns relative reliability across competing CWM candidates from dense trajectory supervision, while APA-CWM provides a training-free analytic reference based on NSG. We also introduced CMC to evaluate localization, trajectory completeness, visibility, and continuity on dynamic tracks. Across DAVIS and Kinetics, LPA-CWM consistently improves over Uniform CWM and APA-CWM under both CMC and TAP-Vid First, while the ablations confirm the contributions of candidate interaction, trajectory supervision, and windowed localization. Three limitations remain. First, LPA can only reweight motion evidence already present in the frozen candidate set, so missing or consistently incorrect candidates cannot be recovered; improving candidate generation or jointly adapting the underlying CWM may address this limitation. Second, LPA is trained on synthetic MOVi-F trajectories, while the current quantitative evaluation covers DAVIS and Kinetics within one CWM family. Extending trajectory supervision to real and embodied data and evaluating across additional datasets and world-model architectures will better establish generalization. Third, multi-mask inference and paired re-evaluation increase per-query computation. More efficient candidate selection, shared computation across masks, and reducing the number of required CWM evaluations are promising directions for lowering this cost. 9References

Frozen candidate generator. The counterfactual predictor and intervention generator remain fixed during LPA training and inference. CWM uses a 32 × 32 patch-grid mask at the 256 × 256 input resolution, where each mask element corresponds to one 8 × 8-pixel image patch; approximately 89.9% of the target-frame patch grid is hidden. Each mask-conditioned counterfactual response is evaluated on a 256 × 256 response grid, and its candidate endpoint is obtained from the peak of the channel-reduced absolute RGB response. Averaging rather than summing over RGB channels changes only a constant factor and therefore does not change the peak location. 

LPA architecture. The complete LPA module contains 3,000,257 trainable parameters. The main text uses height–width–channel notation, whereas the PyTorch implementation stores image-like tensors in channel-first format. The local candidate encoder therefore receives a 10 × 32 × 32 tensor and applies four 3 × 3 convolutional blocks with stride 2 and padding 1, using channel dimensions 

10 → 32 → 64 → 128 → 192 . Each block uses GroupNorm and GELU, followed by global average pooling to produce a 192-dimensional local feature. The global response encoder receives the complete normalized single-channel response in 1 × 32 × 32 

format. It uses four stride-2 convolutional blocks with channel dimensions 1 → 16 → 32 → 64 →

96 , with 3 × 3 kernels, padding 1, GroupNorm, and GELU, followed by global average pooling to produce a 96-dimensional feature. The shared frame-pair encoder receives the concatenated source and target RGB frames in 6 × 64 × 64 format and uses four analogous blocks with channel dimensions 

6 → 32 → 64 → 128 → 192 , producing a 192-dimensional shared context feature. The scalar encoder maps the 16-dimensional candidate descriptor through an MLP with dimensions 

16 → 64 → 64 , followed by GELU and LayerNorm, to obtain a 64-dimensional feature. The four branches are concatenated into a 544-dimensional representation and projected by Linear (544 , 256) ,LayerNorm, and GELU to form a 256-dimensional candidate token. The set encoder contains four pre-normalized Transformer layers with eight attention heads, a 512-dimensional feed-forward block, dropout 0.1, and GELU activation. No candidate-order positional embedding is used. The shared scoring head applies LayerNorm (256) , Linear (256 , 128) , GELU, and Linear (128 , 1) , followed by a softmax over the candidate dimension.

For each frame pair and query, M denotes the number of mask-conditioned candidate responses in the initial CWM set, whereas R denotes the number of paired re-evaluation passes after the initial localization. The two hyperparameters therefore control different stages of inference. When R = 0 , only the initial candidate set is weighted and localized, with no additional CWM pass. For the default R = 1 , the decoder-specific initial endpoint conditions one coarse-conditioned paired re-evaluation. The source location remains the original query, while the initial endpoint determines the center of the heuristic masking applied to the target frame. At 256 × 256 resolution, both source and target retain complete spatial support. A fresh set of M masks is sampled, and the frozen CWM recomputes M mask-conditioned responses. APA and LPA are not rerun, the initial candidate weights are not reused, and the fresh responses are uniformly aggregated. The discrete peak of the resulting response distribution gives the refined endpoint. Thus, the default R = 1 stage is a full-field coarse-conditioned paired re-evaluation rather than a local crop refinement. For R > 1, later passes may become progressively local. The crop side follows sr = s1ηr−1 with fixed η = 0 .75 ; η changes only the crop size and is neither learned nor used in candidate weighting. For non-square inputs, the first square crop uses the shorter image dimension and is shifted to remain inside the valid image region rather than padded. 

Standard and windowed localization. Both decoders start from the same aggregated response distribution using the softmax inverse-temperature parameter βπ = 200 . Standard localization applies no Gaussian-window update and passes the discrete argmax of the unwindowed response distribution to the subsequent R stage. Windowed localization performs two Gaussian-window reweighting steps. At each step, the continuous distribution mean and spatial standard deviation determine the window center and scale, with width 3 max( σ, 0.5) ; the distribution is renormalized after each update. The coordinate passed to the R stage is the discrete argmax after the second update, not the continuous expectation. The two window updates operate within a fixed response field and are distinct from the paired CWM re-evaluation passes. 

Trajectory construction. For each adjacent frame pair, the refined endpoint predicted for the current query is used as the source query in the next pair. The complete post-query trajectory is constructed before any CMC metric is computed. LPA-CWM inherits the CWM response-strength visibility signal and does not train a separate visibility or occlusion head. All compared methods use the same sampled frame sequence, image coordinates, and query identities. 

B Datasets and Evaluation Protocols 

Evaluation datasets. We evaluate on TAP-Vid DAVIS and TAP-Vid Kinetics (Doersch et al., 2022), which provide human-annotated point tracks and visibility labels for object-centric and in-the-wild videos, respectively. DAVIS contains challenging deformation, occlusion, distractors, and fast motion (Pont-Tuset et al., 2017), whereas Kinetics-400 introduces broader variation in actions, camera motion, viewpoint, and human–object interaction (Kay et al., 2017). We use fixed evaluation subsets and identical sampled frames and query tracks for all methods. No model parameter is adapted to either evaluation dataset. 

Evaluation protocol. Following the TAP-Vid First protocol (Doersch et al., 2022), each query is introduced at its first visible frame and evaluated on subsequent frames. Ground-truth coordinates and visibility are taken directly from the TAP-Vid annotations. The same query sets are used for Uniform CWM, APA-CWM, LPA-CWM, and all external baselines, and missing predictions on annotated visible points are retained as failures rather than removed from evaluation. The CWM variants also share the same cached initial response sets; only their candidate weighting differs before the common localization and paired re-evaluation procedure. 

TAP-Vid metrics. We report Average Jaccard, points within the benchmark distance thresholds, mean and median endpoint distance, and occlusion accuracy. Average Jaccard jointly reflects localization and visibility, while the distance metrics separately summarize typical and tail localization errors. CMC uses the same ground-truth tracks but evaluates motion correspondence from the 15 complementary perspectives of localization, completeness, visibility, and temporal continuity, as detailed in Appendix C.

D Adapting External Estimators 

Coordinates. CoTracker3 is evaluated with the official offline point-tracking interface: the full video and first-visible query are provided jointly, and the predictor returns the complete trajectory directly. It therefore requires neither a flow-to-coordinate adapter nor recursive flow propagation. RAFT, SEA-RAFT, DODUO, and SMURF instead predict each post-query coordinate directly from the original query frame. For a query introduced at frame q, each query–target pair (Iq , I t) for 

t > q is evaluated independently, and the forward displacement at the original query location gives the target coordinate. Reverse predictions, when computed, are used only for visibility estimation. GMRW uses its official tracking utility to propagate correspondences through adjacent frames.

Inference-stage ablation. All six configurations use the same frozen CWM, M = 10 , R = 1 ,evaluation seed, DAVIS batches, frame pairs, queries, and initial masks. The rows are Uniform + Std. , APA + Std. , Uniform + Win. , APA + Win. , LPA + Std. , and LPA + Win. . “Std.” uses the discrete peak of the unwindowed aggregated response distribution as the initial endpoint. “Win.” applies two Gaussian-window reweighting updates and uses the discrete peak of the final distribution. Uniform, APA, and LPA respectively denote equal, analytic, and learned weights for the initial candidate set. All six rows then use the same one-pass coarse-conditioned paired re-evaluation after their decoder-specific initial endpoint. The second pass does not rerun APA or LPA, does not reuse the initial weights, and uniformly aggregates the freshly generated responses. This 3 × 2 design therefore separates initial candidate weighting from localization. 

Learned architecture and objective ablations. Objective variants retain the full LPA architecture and compare LKL , LKL + Lcoord , LKL + Lbest , and the full three-term objective.
