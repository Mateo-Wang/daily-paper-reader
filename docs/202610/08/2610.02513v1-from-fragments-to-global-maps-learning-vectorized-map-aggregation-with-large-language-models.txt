## ABSTRACT 

Large-scale vectorized HD maps provide structured road information that is essen-tial for perception, localization, and planning in autonomous driving. Construct-ing such maps requires aggregating noisy, fragmented, and overlapping local pre-dictions collected along a vehicle trajectory into a coherent global map. Existing aggregation methods typically rely on hand-crafted rules for fragment association and refinement. However, a fixed set of thresholds cannot effectively handle varia-tions in road structures and prediction errors, often requiring detector-specific tun-ing or manual adjustment. To address this limitation, we propose MapMergeLLM, a data-driven framework that formulates vectorized map aggregation as condi-tional sequence generation with a large language model. Given serialized local vectorized maps, our model directly predicts the aggregated global map polylines. To reduce dependence on any particular upstream detector, we train the model on synthetic local maps generated from clean vector maps using corruptions that simulate representative prediction errors. We further introduce a coordinate tok-enizer with geometry-aware pretraining to precisely represent map coordinates. In addition, we propose a line-level association loss that explicitly supervises corre-spondences between local observations of the same map element. Experiments on Argoverse2 and nuScenes using multiple recent upstream detectors demonstrate that MapMergeLLM substantially outperforms heuristic and optimization-based aggregation baselines without detector-specific retraining. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

High-definition (HD) maps provide detailed information about road structures to support au-tonomous driving systems (Li et al., 2022; Liao et al., 2024). In vectorized HD maps, line-shaped elements such as lane dividers and road boundaries are represented as polylines, with each polyline represented by an ordered sequence of points. Recent advances in vectorized map construction en-able models to predict these map elements directly from sensor observations (Liu et al., 2023; Qiao et al., 2023a; Yuan et al., 2024; Ding et al., 2023). As onboard sensors only capture a limited region around the ego vehicle, a large-scale HD map cannot be constructed from a single observation, and instead, it must be built from local vectorized predictions collected along a driving sequence. However, simply accumulating these predictions results in redundant, fragmented, and geometrically inconsistent map elements, as the same road structures are repeatedly observed across overlapping local regions. We refer to the process of associating, merging, and refining these local predictions into a coherent global map as vectorized map aggregation . This task is challenging because errors in local map predictions, together with overlapping observations, make it difficult to correctly associate fragments of the same map structure and accurately recover its underlying geometry.

Despite its importance, only a few studies have addressed this problem. Existing methods typically rely on hand-crafted geometric rules with fixed parameters, such as distance thresholds, overlap cri-teria, or sampling intervals (Chen et al., 2023; Qiao et al., 2023b), which may not generalize well across diverse road structures or prediction errors from different upstream detectors. For example, in scenes with dense parallel lanes, noisy observations of one lane may be difficult to distinguish from observations of neighboring lanes. Consider a distance-based association threshold τ , where two fragments are considered for merging if their geometric discrepancy falls below τ . As shown in the top zoomed-in views of Fig. 1(b), a larger τ may incorrectly merge neighboring lanes, whereas a smaller threshold better preserves their distinction but leaves related fragments of the same lane unmerged. Geometric refinement presents a similar trade-off. As shown in the bottom zoomed-in views of Fig. 1(b), geometry-based methods (Cheng et al., 2021; Qiao et al., 2023b) often rely on a fixed fitting interval ∆s that controls how closely the refined line follows local observations. A smaller ∆s allows the line to capture finer geometric variations but also makes it more sensitive to prediction noise, whereas a larger ∆s provides stronger smoothing but may drop important geomet-ric details. In the illustrated example, a larger ∆s better recovers the circular curb but over-smooths other curb structures. These examples show that no single parameter setting is suitable for all road geometry and detection error patterns. The appropriate association threshold or fitting interval de-pends on the local context, such as lane spacing, curvature, and detector noise patterns, and may vary even within the same scene. This motivates us to learn the aggregation process from data, en-abling fragment association and geometric refinement to adapt to the observations rather than being controlled by globally fixed hand-crafted rules. Therefore, we propose MapMergeLLM , which formulates vectorized map aggregation as a condi-tional sequence generation problem and uses a large language model (LLM) (Minaee et al., 2024; Qwen Team, 2026) as the sequence-modeling backbone. Given local vectorized maps from multiple frames, the model directly generates the aggregated global polylines from serialized local fragments. However, training such a model presents two key challenges. First, the model should generalize across different upstream detectors rather than overfit to the error distribution of a particular one. To achieve this, we construct synthetic training data by applying structured corruptions to clean vector maps, simulating common map detection errors such as duplication, fragmentation, and geometric perturbations. Second, as LLMs were originally designed for text generation, adapting them to map generation requires a precise representation of map coordinates. Unlike vanilla tokenization, which may split a numerical value into multiple subword units, we encode each coordinate value as a single token, allowing the model to predict a complete value in a single step. To improve map generation, we propose geometry-aware pretraining that teaches the model numerical and spatial relationships 2Preprint among the new coordinate tokens. We further introduce a line-level association loss that explicitly supervises correspondences between local fragments, helping the model associate observations of the same map element and reduce duplication. Together, these components enable accurate and reliable global map aggregation across different upstream detectors. To demonstrate the effectiveness of MapMergeLLM, we evaluate it on local maps produced by re-cent vectorized map detectors on the Argoverse2 (Wilson et al., 2021) and nuScenes (Caesar et al., 2020) datasets. The experimental results show that MapMergeLLM consistently outperforms heuris-tic and optimization-based aggregation baselines, particularly on road boundaries, where it achieves mean relative F1 improvements of 23.1% on Argoverse2 and 21.9% on nuScenes over the strongest baseline. In summary, our main contributions include: • We identify key limitations of existing heuristic and geometry-based map aggregation methods, showing that fixed hand-crafted merging rules struggle to generalize across di-verse road structures and detector noise patterns. • We propose MapMergeLLM, an LLM-based, data-driven framework for vectorized map aggregation, and introduce coordinate-token pretraining and line-level association loss to improve map generation, supported by detector-agnostic synthetic training data. • We conduct comprehensive experiments on Argoverse2 and nuScenes, demonstrating that MapMergeLLM substantially improves over existing aggregation baselines across multiple recent upstream map detectors. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Vectorized HD Map Construction and Aggregation. Online vectorized HD map construction aims to recover structured map elements, such as lane dividers and road boundaries, within a lo-cal region surrounding the ego vehicle from onboard sensor observations (Li et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023). Early approaches, such as HDMapNet (Li et al., 2022), first predict map elements in the BEV space and then vectorize them, while more recent methods directly predict vectorized maps (Liu et al., 2023; Liao et al., 2023; 2024). Building on this direction, temporal methods further leverage historical observations (Yuan et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024) or a continuously updated global map prior (Shi et al., 2024) to improve prediction consistency. Beyond local map prediction, several works focus on aggregating local vectorized predictions into global maps. VMA (Chen et al., 2023) associates and merges map elements using predefined geometric rules, while MonoLaM (Qiao et al., 2023b) combines geometric association with graph optimization. GNMap (Fan et al., 2024) intro-duces a learning-based fusion framework, but it rasterizes vectorized map tiles before fusion and outputs a semantic map rather than the global polylines required in our task. These aggregation approaches either rely on hand-crafted geometric rules or involve rasterization. In contrast, we pro-pose a data-driven framework that learns to aggregate fragmented observations directly in the vector domain through conditional sequence generation. 

Language Models for Map Generation. The sequence modeling capabilities of LLMs have re-cently been explored for structured geometric prediction, including lane and map generation. Zhang et al. (2026) employs an MLLM with lane-specific tokens to predict lane geometry from visual ob-servations. Yuan et al. (2026) formulates vectorized map construction as autoregressive generation, progressively producing map elements from multi-modal inputs and previously constructed map states. Liang et al. (2026) further extends this paradigm to persistent mapping by jointly model-ing lane geometry, traffic rules, and temporal observations. Another line of work explores the use of vision-language reasoning for map refinement. Xia et al. (2026) uses such reasoning to iden-tify and correct errors in predicted lane-level maps. While these studies demonstrate the potential of language models for structured map generation and refinement, their application to aggregating multiple vectorized map observations remains largely unexplored. 

## 3 METHOD 

We propose MapMergeLLM, a data-driven framework that formulates vectorized map aggregation as a sequence-to-sequence generation task (Section 3.1). To avoid depending on detector-specific er-ror patterns, we construct synthetic training pairs by applying structured corruptions to clean vector 3Preprint maps (Section 3.2). We further introduce coordinate tokenization and a two-stage training scheme (Section 3.3), including geometry-aware pretraining followed by map aggregation fine-tuning. A line-level association loss complements token-level supervision by encouraging association of re-lated line fragments and reducing duplicate map predictions. At inference time, the model takes serialized local predictions in a common coordinate frame as input and generates the global map polylines. We design the detector-agnostic corruption distribution qϕ to model recoverable prediction errors, 

i.e. , errors that can be corrected by aggregating vectorized local observations without access to the original sensor data. Although individual local predictions may be fragmented, duplicated, or geometrically distorted, the overlapping observations should collectively retain sufficient evidence to recover the underlying map element. Therefore, we focus on four representative failure modes: instance duplication, endpoint corruption and fragmentation, unstructured point-wise perturbation, and structured local geometric perturbation. Let Tdup , Tfrag , Tjit , and Tstr denote the four polyline-level corruption operators. Each operator acts on a single polyline and may retain, replace, split, or duplicate the corresponding instance. We lift each operator T to a map-level operator T by independently applying it to each polyline with probability πT , while leaving unselected polylines unchanged. If an operator produces multiple instances, all resulting polylines are retained in the map collection. We then apply the four map-level operators sequentially to generate a synthetic noisy local map as: 

fM(t) = Tstr ◦ T jit ◦ T frag ◦ T dup (Ct(M∗)) . (3) The operators are applied sequentially, allowing multiple failure modes to coexist within a single local map. The parameters of each operator are sampled from predefined ranges, and the resulting composition defines the corruption distribution qϕ.

Instance Duplication. Duplicate detections occur when multiple predicted instances correspond to the same underlying map element. For a selected polyline P, we retain the original instance and generate an additional copy Pdup . The duplicate is resampled along arc length with a randomly varied sampling density, and a subset of its points is perturbed with small zero-mean Gaussian noise to simulate slight geometric differences between duplicate predictions. The duplication operator is defined as 

Tdup (P) = [ P, Pdup ]. (4)

Model Architecture. Given the local vectorized observations defined above, we serialize each map as a sequence of map elements, where each element is represented by its category and the ordered coordinates of its polyline. Standard subword tokenizers, such as byte-pair encoding (BPE) (Sennrich et al., 2016), typically construct tokens based on how frequently character se-quences appear in text, without explicitly considering their numerical meaning. As a result, a single coordinate value may be split into multiple subword units (Kreitner et al., 2026; Singh & Strouse, 2024). For map aggregation, the model must compare coordinates across observations and generate corrected coordinates for polyline merging and refinement. Such fragmentation makes precise co-ordinate generation more difficult, as every token of a coordinate value must be predicted correctly. An error in any token can substantially change the resulting map position. Therefore, we use coordinate tokenization , which encodes each discretized coordinate value as a single new token. Stage 1: Coordinate-Token Pretraining. Although each new coordinate token represents a com-plete coordinate value, the model still needs to learn its numerical meaning and how to use it in map sequences. Map aggregation requires comparing coordinates across observations and adjusting them to recover the underlying geometry. Therefore, we propose a coordinate-token pretraining scheme that uses geometric and arithmetic tasks, such as 2D coordinate translation, midpoint prediction, and point sorting, to help the model learn how to compute and compare coordinates before the model is trained for map aggregation. We also include next-token prediction on serialized local maps to help the model learn how to use the new tokens within polyline sequences. Details of the pretraining tasks are provided in Appendix A.2. During this stage, we optimize only the newly added coordinate-token rows in the input embedding matrix and LM head, while keeping all original pretrained parameters frozen. ## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets. We evaluate MapMergeLLM on the Argoverse2 (Wilson et al., 2021) and nuScenes (Caesar et al., 2020) datasets, focusing on lane dividers and road boundaries. To construct training pairs, we first crop local maps from the ground-truth vectorized maps at intervals of 15m 

7Preprint along the ego trajectory, with each crop covering a 60m × 30m region centered at the correspond-ing ego position. We then apply the synthetic corruption process described in Section 3.2 to these local maps to generate the noisy input observations. The corresponding target global map is defined over the union of these local regions. At inference time, we obtain local map predictions using the official checkpoints of MapTracker (Chen et al., 2024) and StreamMapNet (Yuan et al., 2024). We first transform these predictions from their respective ego coordinate frames into a common global coordinate frame and then use the aligned predictions as input to our model for aggregation. 

Baselines. We compare MapMergeLLM with two representative map aggregation baselines: (1) VMA (Chen et al., 2023) incrementally merges local vectorized maps using predefined association and merging rules, such as checking whether two polylines sufficiently overlap or whether the end-points of each polyline are close to the other. (2) MonoLaM (Qiao et al., 2023b) represents line elements using Catmull-Rom splines. It associates local observations with existing map elements based on geometric criteria and incrementally updates the map through graph optimization. We use the default settings of MonoLaM and adjust the parameters of VMA to better preserve the geometry of the predicted lines. 

Evaluation Metrics. We adopt the line-level matching criterion from MonoLaM to evaluate ag-gregated global maps over complete driving scenes. Following MonoLaM, a predicted polyline is considered a match to a ground-truth polyline if more than 75% of the points from the ground-truth polyline are within a distance threshold ( d) of the prediction. We use greedy matching, where each predicted polyline can be counted as a true positive at most once. We report precision, recall, and F1 scores separately for lane dividers and road boundaries. For each distance threshold, these metrics are computed from counts accumulated over all test scenes. The final results are averaged across d

## 5 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we introduce MapMergeLLM, a data-driven framework for vectorized map aggrega-tion. Instead of relying on predefined association and smoothing rules, our approach formulates map 9Preprint aggregation as a conditional sequence generation problem given the local vectorized map observa-tions. To reduce dependence on specific detectors, we train on synthetic noisy local maps constructed from clean vector maps, where the noise simulates representative detection errors. We further intro-duce coordinate tokenization with geometry-aware coordinate-token pretraining to better represent map elements, as well as a line-level association loss to improve association of map fragments. Experiments on Argoverse2 and nuScenes demonstrate that MapMergeLLM consistently improves over heuristic and optimization-based aggregation baselines across multiple recent upstream detec-tors. Overall, our work highlights data-driven map aggregation as a powerful direction for reducing dependence on heuristic merging rules and improving the robustness of large-scale vectorized map construction. 10 Preprint

A.1.1 TRAINING SETUP 

All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA B200 GPU. We use Qwen3.5-4B as the back-bone and fine-tune it for map aggregation with LoRA, as described in Section 3.3. We apply LoRA to the query and value projections in the attention layers, as well as the gate, up-projection, and down-projection layers in the feed-forward blocks. We use a batch size of 1 with gradient accumu-lation over 8 steps. The learning rate is set to 2 × 10 −4 with a cosine learning-rate schedule and 200 warm-up steps. We optimize the model using AdamW with a weight decay of 0.01. The checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is used for evaluation. A.1.2 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS

We construct synthetic geometric and arithmetic tasks using 2D coordinates represented by the new coordinate tokens. The tasks include 2D coordinate translation, point-wise subtraction, midpoint prediction, point sorting, nearest-point selection, and bounding-box corner prediction. Translation adds a displacement to a 2D point, while subtraction predicts the displacement between two points. Midpoint prediction computes the coordinate-wise average of two points, providing supervision for an operation relevant to merging nearby observations. Point sorting orders points first by y and then by x, encouraging the model to learn a consistent spatial ordering. Nearest-point selection deter-mines which of two candidate points is closer to a query point, providing supervision for geometric distance comparison. Bounding-box corner prediction returns the minimum and maximum coordi-nates of a polyline, capturing its spatial extent. Each task is defined by an operation and its input coordinates, followed by the correct answer. We encode all coordinates using the new tokens and apply the training loss only to the answer tokens. We also pretrain the model on serialized local maps from the training scenes using next-token pre-diction. Given the current sequence prefix, the model predicts the next token in the serialized map sequence. This task is important, as it helps the model learn how the coordinate tokens are used within polyline sequences, which complements the numerical and spatial relationships learned from the geometric and arithmetic tasks. Since this pretraining task only involves local map observations, it does not require any ground-truth global maps.

In the main paper, our experimental results demonstrate that vectorized map aggregation can be learned from synthetic local observations, which reduces the dependence on predefined association and merging rules. By combining the proposed coordinate-token pretraining with line-level associ-ation supervision, MapMergeLLM improves aggregation across different upstream detectors on Ar-goverse2 and nuScenes datasets. These results also motivate further study of data-driven approaches for larger maps and other related applications. 

Limitations and Future Work. Our experiments demonstrate effective aggregation over individ-ual driving scenes. Extending this framework to longer driving trajectories is a natural direction for future work. However, this could be challenging because the growing number of local observations can exceed the context window of LLMs. Compared with standard subword tokenization, our co-ordinate tokenization already alleviates this limitation by representing each coordinate value as a single token, which allows more map observations to fit within the same context window. Incremen-tal aggregation over overlapping regions is a promising future direction for handling longer driving trajectories while keeping each input sequence within the model’s context window. The coordinate resolution of 0.1m used in our experiments provides enough spatial detail and well supports the aggregation results reported on Argoverse2 and nuScenes. Adopting a finer resolution or extending the spatial range would further increase the vocabulary size.
