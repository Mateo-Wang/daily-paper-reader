Abstract—Interactive world simulators can provide scalable
environments for robot planning, policy training, and evaluation
by predicting action consequences while reducing reliance on
repeated physical rollouts. To serve these applications, they
must generate future image sequences that respond faithfully
to robot actions and preserve the dynamics of robot-object
interactions over long horizons. However, existing world models
typically predict the entire next latent state and often fail to
capture subtle changes induced by robot actions. Such omis-
sions can produce physically implausible outcomes, including
object interpenetration and excessive deformation. To address
this limitation, we propose DeltaWorld, a physically consis-
tent interactive world simulator for robotic manipulation. Our
method introduces the Delta Latent Transition Model (Delta-
LTM), which predicts action-induced latent feature changes and
adds them to the current latent state to obtain the next state,
rather than predicting the next latent state directly. To mitigate
object interpenetration and excessive deformation in predicted
future frames, Interaction-aware Latent Alignment is introduced
to construct counterfactual interaction regions and supervise
interaction-related latent changes. DeltaWorld is evaluated on
the IWS manipulation benchmark and a self-collected cross-robot
dataset covering multiple robot embodiments and manipulation
tasks. On the cross-robot dataset, DeltaWorld reduces FVD by
46.6% and LPIPS by 31.1% relative to the IWS baseline. These
results highlight the potential of DeltaWorld for long-horizon
action-conditioned video prediction in robotic manipulation.
Index Terms—World models, robot manipulation, latent dy-
namics, action-conditioned video prediction, physical consistency.
I. INTRODUCTION
W
ITH the increasing adoption of data-driven approaches
in robotic manipulation, real-world robot interaction
data have become a fundamental resource for learning com-
plex manipulation behaviors [1]–[5]. However, policy training,
action exploration, and performance evaluation often require
repeated physical robot rollouts. These rollouts consume time
and hardware resources, limiting the scale and reproducibility
of experiments [1], [6]. World models offer a promising way
to reduce reliance on physical rollouts by learning action-
conditioned environment dynamics from existing robot in-
teraction data. Such models can simulate the outcomes of
robot actions, thereby supporting planning [7], [8], policy
learning, and evaluation [6], [9], [10]. Unlike conventional

image prediction models that primarily focus on visual appear-
ance changes, world models for robotic manipulation place
greater emphasis on action-conditioned environment evolution,
including object motion, robot–object interactions, and long-
horizon action consequences [11].
To realize action-conditioned prediction, recent work has
explored different representations of future robot observations.
JEPA-style models forecast learned visual features without
directly reconstructing pixels [12], [13]. The Interactive World
Simulator (IWS) learns action-conditioned dynamics in a
compact image latent space and decodes predicted states into
video [6]. In parallel, video-generative world models support
interactive evaluation by conditioning future frames on policy-
derived latent actions [14] or on frame-level controls with
pose-conditioned memory [10]. More recently, dWorldEval
has modeled observations and actions jointly as discrete to-
kens, with sparse keyframes to support long-horizon predic-
tion [15].
Despite these advances, predicting robot manipulation
videos remains difficult when an action changes only a small
part of the scene. Most of the image may stay the same
between adjacent frames, while a robot action can move an
object or alter its shape. Models that directly predict future
frames or their latent representations may capture the largely
unchanged scene while missing small changes to manipulated
objects. The resulting errors can grow during autoregressive
rollout and cause object drift or implausible deformation.
Therefore, the key challenge is to predict the changes caused
by robot actions, especially in regions where the robot interacts
with objects.
To address this challenge, we propose DeltaWorld, a phys-
ically consistent interactive world simulator that predicts
action-induced latent changes instead of the entire next latent
state. Our method introduces the Delta Latent Transition
Model (Delta-LTM), which estimates these changes and adds
them to the current latent state to obtain the next state.
Interaction-aware Latent Alignment uses counterfactual in-
teraction regions to emphasize changes around robot–object
interactions during training. Experiments on the IWS bench-
mark and a self-collected cross-robot dataset show improved
action-conditioned video prediction quality and long-horizon
consistency across multiple manipulation tasks and robot em-
bodiments. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
1) We propose DeltaWorld, an interactive world simulator

II. RELATED WORKS
A. World Models for Robotic Manipulation
World models have recently emerged as an important re-
search direction in robot learning. By learning predictive mod-
els of how the environment evolves under robot actions, robots
can internally simulate possible future outcomes, enabling
applications such as planning, control, policy steering, and
policy evaluation [6]–[10]. Early and classical world-modeling
approaches typically learn environment dynamics in compact
state spaces and use the learned models for model-based deci-
sion making and control [16], [17]. More recently, advances in
visual generative modeling have motivated world models that
learn action-conditioned environment dynamics directly from
high-dimensional visual observations, often through compact
latent representations [6], [7], [18].
Existing visual world models have mainly developed along
two representative directions. One line of work leverages
visual priors learned by large-scale video generation models
to support robotic planning and interaction through future
video or visual trajectory generation [8], [19]–[21]. Another
line builds action-conditioned predictive models directly from
robot interaction data, allowing generated observations to ex-
plicitly respond to robot actions and further support interactive
rollouts, policy steering, and policy evaluation [6], [10], [22].
These studies indicate that robotic world models are gradually
evolving from open-ended visual generation toward interactive
environment models that can capture action consequences
and interaction outcomes. Nevertheless, effectively modeling
action-driven environment state changes in a compact repre-
sentation space remains a key challenge for achieving stable
long-horizon prediction and physically consistent interaction.
B. Action-conditioned Latent Dynamics

studies have explored action-conditioned dynamics in compact
latent spaces. These methods typically map visual observations
into latent states using pretrained visual encoders, autoen-
coders, or joint-embedding models, and predict future states
from historical latent representations and robot actions [6], [7],
[23], [24]. For example, DINO-WM [7] directly predicts future
patch-level representations based on pretrained visual features,
thereby avoiding explicit pixel reconstruction. IWS [6] adopts
a two-stage framework, where an action-conditioned latent dy-
namics model is learned after freezing the visual autoencoder.
Recent studies have further investigated whether latent
dynamics representations truly preserve information relevant
to control and interaction. One line of work employs joint-
embedding prediction [25] or task-relevant representation
learning [12] to encourage future latent representations to
focus more on state information useful for planning [26].
Another line of work further leverages predicted latent states,
latent subgoals, or action-relevant representations for action
generation and policy learning [27]–[31]. These studies sug-
gest that, compared with directly generating future observa-
tions at the pixel level, modeling action-conditioned environ-
ment evolution in latent space [32] can provide more com-
pact and decision-relevant dynamics representations. However,
most existing methods still take future latent states or future
visual representations as their primary prediction targets, while
explicitly modeling latent state transitions induced by robot
actions has received comparatively less attention. In contrast,
we focus on the action-conditioned latent transition itself and
explicitly model this transition in interaction-relevant regions.
C. World Models for Policy Training and Evaluation
Beyond predicting future environment evolution, world
models can also serve as interactive surrogate environments
that provide scalable data sources for robot policy training. Im-
itation learning and vision-language-action policies typically
rely on large amounts of high-quality real-robot demonstra-
tions, while collecting such data is costly, hardware-dependent,
and difficult to scale rapidly. To alleviate this bottleneck, recent
studies have begun to use world models as interactive environ-
ments for data generation and policy optimization, supporting
synthetic interaction trajectory generation, data augmentation,
and world-model-based policy training and post-training [33]–
[39]. IWS further demonstrates that demonstrations can be
collected directly within a learned interactive world model
to support the training of multiple imitation learning policies
without requiring additional real-robot interaction [6].
Another important application of world models is robot
policy evaluation. Real-world evaluation often requires re-
peated environment resets, human supervision, and substantial
hardware runtime, while strictly controlling initial conditions
across experiments is difficult. These factors make large-
scale checkpoint selection and policy comparison challenging.
To address this issue, an increasing number of studies have
explored action-conditioned video world models as surro-
gates for the real environment, estimating policy performance
through closed-loop interaction between the policy and the
generated environment [14], [15], [40]–[42]. Works such as
WorldEval [14], WorldGym [40], and dWorldEval [15] sug-
gest that world-model-based evaluation can, to some extent,
preserve the relative performance ordering among different
policies or model checkpoints, while providing a scalable and

In Stage 2, the encoder and decoder are frozen while an
action-conditioned dynamics model is trained to predict the
latent feature increment induced by robot actions. Given the
latest N latent states and the corresponding action sequence,
the model estimates the change from the current to the next
latent state rather than generating the entire next state. The
predicted increment is added to the current state, and this
update is repeated autoregressively to produce future latent
states. By carrying largely unchanged scene features forward,
the residual update focuses learning on action-driven object
motion and robot-object interactions.
Although Delta-LTM makes action-conditioned latent incre-
ments the primary prediction target, not all latent changes are
equally relevant to robot-object interaction. Changes related
to robot interaction typically occupy only a small portion of
the latent representation, while most latent locations mainly
encode static objects or background appearance. Therefore,
applying uniform supervision across all latent locations may
weaken the learning signals associated with robot–object inter-
actions. To address this issue, we further propose Interaction-
aware Latent Alignment. Specifically, we construct a counter-
factual interaction map by comparing the latent representations
of the original observation and a corresponding counterfactual
observation in which the manipulated object and the robot
arm regions involved in the current interaction are removed.
This interaction map identifies latent regions that are sensitive
to robot–object interactions and assigns greater weight to the
corresponding state transitions during dynamics learning.
During inference, the simulator starts from observed la-
tent states and autoregressively predicts future latent states
conditioned on a sequence of robot actions. Each predicted
latent state is decoded by the frozen decoder to generate the
corresponding RGB observation, which is then used as context
for subsequent predictions. In this way, DeltaWorld supports
long-horizon interactive rollouts under robot action condition-
ing and provides an interactive predictive environment for
downstream robotic world-model applications.
B. Delta Latent Transition Model
Action-conditioned world models aim to capture how robot
actions transform the current environment state into the next
one. We therefore formulate the action-induced latent incre-
ment as the explicit prediction target of the dynamics model,
rather than treating it implicitly within complete future-state
prediction. This formulation is particularly suitable for robotic
manipulation: much of the visual scene changes little between
adjacent frames and the action-induced dynamics are reflected
in robot motion, object displacement, contact changes, and
interaction responses. We introduce the Delta Latent Transition
Model (Delta-LTM), which estimates the latent increment
from the current state and robot actions and applies it to update
the current state. In this way, the model learns how robot
actions change the latent state rather than directly predicting
the complete future state.

their latent-space feature increment as

object position or contact. To address this issue, we introduce
Interaction-aware Latent Alignment, which uses counterfactual
interaction supervision to identify latent regions associated
with robot-object interactions and assigns greater weight to

IV. EXPERIMENTS
In this section, we evaluate DeltaWorld from three per-
spectives: 1) How does DeltaWorld compare with state-of-
the-art world models in terms of action-conditioned video
prediction quality and long-horizon interaction consistency?
2) Can the proposed latent increment formulation remain
effective across different robot embodiments and manipulation
tasks? 3) How does each proposed component contribute to
the overall performance? We first introduce the experimental
setup in Section IV-A. Section IV-B presents quantitative and
qualitative comparisons on both the IWS benchmark and our
self-collected cross-robot dataset, while Section IV-C provides
ablation studies of Delta-LTM and Interaction-aware Latent
Alignment.
A. World Model Instantiation
We instantiate DeltaWorld on five robotic manipulation
tasks from the Interactive World Simulator (IWS) benchmark,
including T-pushing, bimanual box manipulation, bimanual
sweeping, bimanual rope manipulation, and single-arm grasp-
ing. These tasks cover rigid-object manipulation, deformable-
object interaction, as well as both single-arm and bimanual
manipulation settings. For each task, the training set contains
approximately 500–600 interaction episodes. Each dataset pro-
vides two camera views. Following the experimental protocol
of IWS, we train and evaluate independent world models for
different camera views.
To further evaluate the applicability of DeltaWorld across
different robot platforms, we additionally collect four manip-
ulation tasks using three different robot platforms, including
whiteboard wiping, chip grasping, cable arrangement, and Peg-
in-Hole. The corresponding training sets contain 127, 329,
886, and 893 episodes, respectively, covering different robot
embodiments, action spaces, and forms of robot–object inter-
action. Unlike the IWS datasets, the interaction trajectories
in our self-collected datasets have task-dependent variable
lengths, preserving the natural variation in trajectory duration

B. Video Baseline Comparisons
To evaluate the performance of DeltaWorld on action-
conditioned video prediction, we compare it with several
representative world models, including LeWorld [23], Cos-
mos3 [19], and IWS [6]. All methods use the same data splits
and evaluation protocol. We report FID, FVD, LPIPS, PSNR,
SSIM, and MSE to comprehensively evaluate visual quality,
perceptual consistency, and temporal prediction performance.
For the baseline models, LeWorld and IWS are trained
from scratch following their publicly released implementations
and default training configurations, with the corresponding
method-specific hyperparameter settings. Cosmos 3 provides
an official fine-tuning pipeline for robotic data. We therefore
first convert the robot interaction data into the required format
and then fine-tune the model using the default Cosmos 3 fine-
tuning settings. All methods are trained using the same training
data and validation splits and are evaluated under a consistent
rollout setting.
We first compare the methods on five robotic manipulation
tasks from the IWS benchmark under two camera views.

C. Ablation Study
To analyze the contributions of the Delta Latent Transition
Model (Delta-LTM) and Interaction-aware Latent Alignment
to the overall model performance, we conduct ablation studies
by removing one component at a time while keeping all
other training and evaluation settings unchanged. Table III
summarizes the average results on the IWS benchmark and
our self-collected cross-robot dataset.
The complete DeltaWorld achieves the best overall perfor-
mance across all evaluation metrics. Removing Delta-LTM
leads to clear degradation in FVD, LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM, and
MSE. In particular, FVD increases from 191.50 to 228.03,
indicating that directly predicting future absolute latent states
weakens the model’s ability to capture long-horizon dynamics.
Removing Interaction-aware Latent Alignment also degrades
performance across the evaluated metrics, with consistent
drops in FVD, LPIPS, and reconstruction-quality measures.
This suggests that strengthening supervision over latent re-
gions associated with robot–object interactions further im-
proves perceptual consistency and interaction stability.

V. CONCLUSION
In this work, we presented DeltaWorld, a physically consis-
tent interactive world simulator for robotic world models. In-
stead of treating the complete future latent state as the primary
prediction target, the Delta Latent Transition Model (Delta-
LTM) estimates action-conditioned latent increments and ap-
plies them to update the current state. Interaction-aware Latent
Alignment further emphasizes transition errors in robot–object
interaction-sensitive regions through counterfactual supervi-
sion. Together, these two components provide an increment-
based and interaction-aware formulation for learning robotic
world dynamics. Experiments on the IWS manipulation bench-
mark and a self-collected multi-robot dataset demonstrate
improved action-conditioned video prediction across image-
and video-level metrics, while ablation studies confirm the
complementary roles of the two components.
