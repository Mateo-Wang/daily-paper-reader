# Controllable and Photorealistic Pedestrian Risky Motion Generation for End-to-End Driving Safety Evaluation 

Siyuan Liu 1,2, Miao Li 2, Haibao Yu 2, Haohong Lin 2, Qing Zhou 1, Bingbing Nie 1,3,† and Ding Zhao 2

Project Page: https://controlped.netlify.app 

Abstract — Evaluating end-to-end autonomous driving un-der rare, safety-critical vehicle-pedestrian interactions requires photorealistic, sensor-level scenarios. However, trajectory-based scenario generators cannot synthesize raw visual observations, whereas video-based approaches lack controllability. To bridge this gap, we present ControlPed, a novel framework that combines trajectory-level conflict synthesis with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to generate photorealistic, motion-controllable safety-critical scenarios. Built upon HazardPed, a dataset derived from 10,352 traffic videos comprising 422 conflict trajectories, HD maps, and 857 annotated 3D human motions, ControlPed first generates conflict trajectories, lifts them into 3D human motion sequences via text-conditioned motion diffu-sion, and finally renders multi-view sensor observations using animatable 3DGS avatars. Safety evaluation in 88 rendered photorealistic scenarios reveals that seven leading end-to-end driving models suffer a severe performance drop, with their mean HDScore plunging from 88.8 to 47.4, exposing major failure modes under dangerous pedestrian behaviors. The dataset and testing benchmarks will be released to facilitate safety assessment of vehicle-pedestrian interactions. 

I. INTRODUCTION Evaluating end-to-end autonomous driving requires sensor-level safety-critical data, since raw observations are directly mapped to future trajectories [1]. However, high-risk vehicle-pedestrian interactions are rare in real-world driving logs. This motivates synthesizing vehicle-pedestrian conflict scenarios at the sensor-level to evaluate models under realistic deployment conditions [2]. Existing approaches for safety-critical scenario genera-tion follow trajectory-based or video-based paradigms. For trajectory-based methods, adversarial [3] methods often gen-erate unnatural trajectories, and learning-based [4] methods suffer from data scarcity. Both of them lack multi-view video rendering. In contrast, video-based methods satisfy sensor-level requirements [5], [6], but lack behavioral controllability. Recent technical advances provide new ways to address these challenges. 3DGS converts driving logs into photorealistic scenes [7], text-conditioned motion generation models enable fine-grained control [8], and animatable avatars facilitate dynamic integration [9]. A. Safety-Critical Scenario Generation 

Trajectory-based scenario generators synthesize hazardous interactions via behavior perturbation [3], [12] or traffic priors [4]. However, their 2D bird’s-eye-view representations strip away key pedestrian dynamics essential for vision-based end-to-end models. Conversely, video-based approaches in-troduce sensor-level realism via asset insertion or pose-guided editing [5], [6], yet lack fine-grained trajectory or human control. Asset insertion struggles to enforce pre-cise terminal collision states, while 2D pose editing lacks metric grounding, preventing control over real-world vehi-cle–pedestrian distances. Consequently, achieving joint con-trollability over macro trajectories and micro pedestrian mo-tions in photorealistic rendering remains an open challenge. 

B. Human Motion Generation and Datasets 

Text-conditioned human motion generation models, built on diffusion architectures [8], [11], synthesize diverse mo-tions from language but lack spatial grounding to specify coordinate dynamics. Although recent spatial control tech-niques incorporate trajectory or sparse joint constraints [13]– [15], their applicability remains bounded by the underlying training data. Standard motion benchmarks built on AMASS, HumanML3D, and BABEL focus almost exclusively on daily activities [16]. Consequently, critical conflict dynamics, such as abrupt dodging or freezing under imminent vehicle threat, remain remarkably scarce, hindering the generation of safety-critical scenarios. 

C. Scenario Reconstruction and End-to-End Evaluation 

3DGS enables photorealistic rendering and has been widely adopted in driving scenario reconstruction [17]. To handle dynamic objects, recent approaches decouple fore-ground actors from background modeling, allowing moving entities to be controlled independently [7]. Advances in pose-driven animatable avatars further free pedestrians from rigid log replaying, enabling the insertion of hazardous human behaviors into reconstructed scenes. For safety evaluation, existing paradigms face clear trade-offs: open-loop tests on passive logs lack reactivity, simulators exhibit a pronounced sim-to-real gap. Recently, reconstruction-based closed-loop simulations combine realism with interactivity by evaluating models on rendered real-world scenes [2], yet they lack criti-cal pedestrian-involved hazards. Therefore, injecting control-lable pedestrian behaviors into photorealistic reconstructed scenes is essential for end-to-end safety evaluation. III. B. Human Motion Generation 

Task Definition. We formulate trajectory-conditioned hu-man motion generation as lifting a 2D trajectory {pτ }Tτ =1 

into motion x = {xτ }Tτ =1 aligned with the input trajectory. At each frame, the motion state xτ ∈ R135 stacks the root translation tτ , root orientation ϕτ in 6D representation, and body joint rotations θτ across 21 joints in 6D. The synthesis is guided by the action category a ∈ A , the corresponding text prompt y, and the trajectory {pτ }Tτ =1 . Rather than encoding the trajectory through an external feature extractor, we directly assign {pτ }Tτ =1 to the first two channels of 

xτ and keep them clamped throughout generation. Thus, motion synthesis is formulated as an inpainting task where the denoiser completes the remaining 133 channels. 

Motion Diffusion with Trajectory Anchoring. Unlike trajectory generation, our motion synthesis directly predicts clean motion ˆx = F (xk, k, cm) from noisy motion xk at the diffusion step k over a noising chain {xk}Kk=0 . During diffusion, the root horizontal channels are overwritten by the input trajectory at every step and excluded from loss com-putation. To factorize motion control, the root heading ψτ

is derived by smoothing the differentiation of the trajectory, leaving the network to predict only the residual orientation: 

Rres = Rz (−ψτ ) Rfull , (5) where Rfull ∈ SO (3) is the absolute orientation of the root and Rz (·) denotes rotation about the vertical axis. At the output, the absolute orientation is recomposed as 

Rfull = Rz (ψτ )Rres , ensuring that the network focuses on body turns relative to the path rather than the global direction. The backbone is an eight-layer pre-LN transformer with a hidden dimension of 512 and 8 attention heads. The motion condition vector cm ∈ R512 sums the diffusion time embedding, the frozen CLIP text embedding of y, and the learned action category embedding of a, modulating every sublayer via adaLN-Zero [26]:

rescales and shifts normalized activations, while α gates the sublayer residual output. Modulating every sublayer increases the condition at every depth, preventing the control signal from being diluted as it propagates. The model is trained by minimizing a composite loss: 

Lm = Lrot + λv Lv + λpLp + λcLc + λz Lz + λsLs, (7) where λ(·) are the balance weights. Lrot measures the recon-struction error across the 133 generated unclamped channels, while Lz and Ls upweight root height and spine orientation channels to prevent feature dilution. The remaining terms enforce kinematic fidelity in world space via forward kine-matics. Lp and Lv penalize 3D joint position and interframe velocity errors, while Lc suppresses foot sliding during ground contact phases. During inference, denoising begins from gaussian noise with root horizontal channels clamped to the reference 2D trajectory. The text prompt y and the action category a

are jointly dropped to enable classifier-free guidance. Long motion sequences are synthesized by conditioning each slid-ing window on a historical frame prefix. Navigation texts are assigned segment-wise according to the curvature of the generated hazardous trajectory, and a reaction motion sampled from the hazardous categories is concatenated at the conflict point. Linear blending is then applied across the window seams to ensure kinematic continuity.

End-to-End Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate keyframe-planned trajectories using HUGSim [2] metrics: no collision (NC ), drivable area compliance ( DAC ), time-to-collision (TTC ), comfort ( COM ), route completion ( RC ), and their composite HDScore . Unlike closed-loop evaluation, we de-sign an open-loop proxy for RC , terminating the route at the first keyframe where a collision occurs within 0.5 s. 

Baselines. We compare trajectory generation with Adv-BMT [3], CCDiff [4], and CounterScene [12]. For motion generation, we evaluate MDM [8], CondMDI [13], Stable-MoFusion [11], PriorMDM [15], and OmniControl [14], all retrained in HazardPed under identical data splits. For end-to-end evaluation, UniAD [1] and VAD [27] employ modular dense BEV representations, SparseDrive [28] and SSR [29] utilize sparse query representations, LAW [30] incorporates an implicit world model, DiffusionDrive [31] adopts a diffusion policy, and LTF [32] serves as a vision-only baseline. All end-to-end models are evaluated open-loop using official pre-trained weights without fine-tuning.

C. End-to-End Safety Evaluation 

Evaluating seven end-to-end driving models under ren-dered pedestrian hazards reveals a severe average HDScore 

drop from 88 .8 to 47 .4 (Table V). Performance degrada-tion is highly metric-specific. The drivable area compliance (DAC ) remains largely unaffected, showing that planners preserve drivable space awareness. Instead, the score collapse is primarily driven by sharp declines in route completion (RC ), no collision ( NC ), and time-to-collision ( TTC ). This indicates that models do not lose structural road discipline in pedestrian conflicts. Rather, they fail to resolve critical interaction dynamics, causing collisions that prematurely terminate route execution.
