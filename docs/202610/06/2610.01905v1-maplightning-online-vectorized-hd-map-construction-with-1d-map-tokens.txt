## ABSTRACT 

Online vectorized HD map construction is essential for scaling safe autonomous driving and requires accurate, real-time inference. Prior methods typically rely on dense bird’s-eye-view (BEV) grids as the intermediate representation. We propose 

MapLightning , which replaces the dense BEV grid with a compact set of 1D learn-able map tokens. To construct map tokens from image features, we choose self-attention over vanilla cross-attention because it enables joint interactions and con-textual aggregation among image and map tokens. Our transformer-based mapper concatenates map and image tokens, applies full self-attention, discards the image tokens, and retains the updated map tokens for decoding. This design offers three advantages. First, our representation is efficient, using fewer tokens, consuming less memory, and running faster. Second, the lightweight design allows the map decoder to use full rather than deformable cross-attention for better global context. Third, unlike BEV-based methods, our network does not use camera projection parameters, making it robust to camera-extrinsic perturbations. MapLightning uses up to 16.7 × fewer intermediate tokens than dense BEV-based methods and achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and efficiency on nuScenes and Argoverse 2. Its lightweight variant surpasses MapTRv2 by +10.1 mAP on nuScenes and +16.2 mAP on Argoverse 2, while delivering 1.73 × faster inference (40+ FPS) with 53% less memory. We further show improvements on uncertainty-aware map construc-tion and downstream trajectory prediction. Code and models will be released. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

High-definition (HD) maps provide centimeter-level vectorized representations of static road ele-ments such as lane dividers, pedestrian crossings, and road boundaries. These elements encode road topology and driving constraints essential for autonomous navigation. Traditionally, HD maps are constructed offline using SLAM- or SfM-based pipelines (Zhang et al., 2014; Shan & Englot, 2018; Zhanabatyrova et al., 2024), requiring expensive annotation and maintenance. Recently, online vectorized HD map construction (Liao et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Liao et al., 2025; Dong et al., 2025; Monninger et al., 2026) has gained popularity by building local maps at runtime from onboard sensors, reducing reliance on costly offline map construction and maintenance. Online vectorized HD map construction methods typically follow a three-stage pipeline: an image encoder extracts features from surround-view images, a bird’s-eye-view (BEV) projection module such as LSS (Philion & Fidler, 2020) or BEVFormer (Li et al., 2024b) projects them into a dense BEV grid, and a DETR-style (Carion et al., 2020) decoder with deformable atten-tion (Zhu et al., 2020) predicts map elements as ordered point sets. The BEV-based design is effective for dense spatial perception tasks like semantic segmenta-tion (Philion & Fidler, 2020; Zhou & Kr¨ ahenb¨ uhl, 2022) and 3D occupancy prediction (Yu et al., 2023; Hou et al., 2024), where dense spatial prediction naturally aligns with the BEV grid. How-ever, it is less suited for vectorized map prediction, where the output is a sparse set of vectorized map elements that occupy only 4.27% of the rasterized perception range on nuScenes (Caesar et al., 2020) and 2.65% on Argoverse 2 (Wilson et al., 2023). In this paper, we propose to use 1D learnable tokens, which we call map tokens , as the intermediate scene representation. This design offers three key advantages. (i) Our representation uses fewer intermediate tokens, consumes less memory, and runs faster. Constructing map tokens requires compressing a large number of multi-view image tokens into a much smaller set, and the choice of compression mechanism is critical. Specifically, we concatenate map and image tokens and apply attention over the joint sequence. As shown in Figure 1, we adopt self-attention instead of vanilla cross-attention, since vanilla cross-attention only lets map tokens read from image tokens, whereas self-attention also allows explicit image-to-image and map-to-map token interactions for richer contextual aggregation. The image-to-image interactions are particularly useful for refining visual context: as illustrated in Figure 3, self-attention progressively concentrates on map-relevant structures across layers. After the final mapper layer, the image tokens are discarded, leaving compact map tokens that retain the aggregated map-relevant information. These map tokens serve as the intermediate representation for the decoder. In the decoder, cross-attention is used because the map queries act as output slots that read from the fixed map-token representation, similar to decoding objects (Carion et al., 2020) or categories (Strudel et al., 2021). We name our framework MapLightning for its fast inference, token-efficient design, and high ac-curacy. By replacing the dense BEV grid with a compact set of tokens (up to 16.7 × fewer), Map-Lightning achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and efficiency on nuScenes and Argoverse 2. With a lightweight MobileNetV3-Large backbone, it surpasses MapTRv2 (Liao et al., 2025) by +10.1 and 

+16.2 mAP on nuScenes and Argoverse 2, running 1.73 × faster ( 40+ FPS ) with 53% less memory. In summary, the main contributions of this paper are: • We propose MapLightning, the first online vectorized HD map construction framework that re-places dense BEV projection with attention-based mapping into compact 1D map tokens, yielding higher efficiency, better global context, and robustness to extrinsic perturbations. • We compare cross-attention and self-attention for constructing map tokens from image features, and show that self-attention is more effective for online vectorized HD map construction. • MapLightning outperforms prior methods on online vectorized HD map construction, uncertainty-aware map construction, downstream trajectory prediction, and 3D object detection. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 ONLINE VECTORIZED HD M AP CONSTRUCTION 

Most online vectorized HD map construction methods follow a MapTR-style (Liao et al., 2022) framework with dense BEV representations and a DETR-style decoder (Liu et al., 2023b; Liao 2et al., 2022; 2025). Follow-up works refine the decoder, supervision, and temporal modeling within this paradigm (Yuan et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2025; Monninger et al., 2026). Our approach differs from prior online vectorized HD map construction methods in two key aspects: 

(i) Instead of following the conventional dense BEV paradigm, we compress image tokens into compact 1D map tokens using attention, achieving higher efficiency and accuracy. (ii) Unlike many previous methods that focus on map construction alone, we show that our representation benefits downstream trajectory prediction and generalizes to 3D object detection. 2.2 BEYOND BEV-B ASED REPRESENTATION 

BEV representations have been widely adopted for dense spatial perception. For example, Lift-Splat-Shoot (Philion & Fidler, 2020) and Cross-View Transformers (Zhou & Kr¨ ahenb¨ uhl, 2022) construct BEV features for semantic segmentation, whereas FlashOcc (Yu et al., 2023) and Fas-tOcc (Hou et al., 2024) extend this representation to 3D occupancy prediction. Works on sparse prediction have explored removing BEV as an intermediate representation by ag-gregating image features into task queries. In 3D object detection, SparseBEV (Liu et al., 2023a) achieves performance comparable to dense-BEV methods like BEVFormer v2 (Yang et al., 2023). In online vectorized HD map construction, SparseMeXt (Jiang et al., 2025) follows a similar direction but underperforms dense-BEV methods like MapTRv2 (Liao et al., 2025) in comparable settings. Unlike prior methods that either retain BEV-based feature projection or drop the intermediate repre-sentation, we use 1D learnable map tokens as a compact and performant intermediate representation. 2.3 COMPRESSING TOKENS VIA ATTENTION MECHANISMS 

Learned query tokens are a common pattern for pooling a large set of input tokens via cross-attention. Queries can decode specific outputs, such as object predictions (Carion et al., 2020) or slots that explain an image (Locatello et al., 2020; Vani et al., 2024). Queries can also compress tokens into a smaller bottleneck for further processing, as in Perceiver’s latents (Jaegle et al., 2021; 2022), Flamingo’s resampler (Alayrac et al., 2022), and Q-Former (Li et al., 2023a). Learned tokens can also aggregate information through self-attention by being concatenated with input tokens and jointly updated within the same sequence, which most closely resembles our design. Such tokens have been explored in general representation learning and sequence modeling, including the [CLS] token in BERT (Devlin et al., 2019), memory tokens (Burtsev et al., 2020), the [CLS] 

token in ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), gist tokens (Mu et al., 2023), summary vectors (Chevalier et al., 2023), register tokens in Registers (Darcet et al., 2024), and planning tokens in Flex (Yang et al., 2025a). They have also been used in dense or generative tasks, such as latent tokens in LVSM (Jin et al., 2024) and light tokens in RelitLRM (Zhang et al., 2025). Unlike previous works targeting dense or general-purpose perception, we apply attention-based to-ken compression to the sparse task of online vectorized HD map construction with a tailored mapper.

We retain the decoder architecture of MapTRv2 (Liao et al., 2025), including its Ld = 6 layers. We change what the decoder attends to: the compact map tokens U ∈ RK×d instead of the dense BEV features B ∈ RNBEV ×d. This change affects the decoder in two ways. First, since U has no fixed 2D coordinates, deformable attention, which samples features around BEV reference points, no longer applies. Second, since K ≪ NBEV (1,200 map tokens versus 20 ,000 BEV tokens on nuScenes, 

16.7 × fewer), full cross-attention becomes feasible, letting each query attend to all map tokens. Following MapTRv2 (Liao et al., 2025), each decoder layer first applies decoupled self-attention to the queries along the instance and point dimensions, yielding Q(l). We then replace deformable cross-attention with full cross-attention to the map tokens:

Prediction head. We retain MapTRv2’s iterative coordinate refinement. Initial reference points are predicted from the decoder queries via a linear projection and sigmoid. At each layer, the regression branch predicts offsets that refine the current reference points for the next layer, while the classifica-tion branch predicts the class of each map element. Unlike MapTRv2, these reference points serve only for coordinate regression, not feature sampling. The final normalized coordinates are converted to metric positions as described in Section 3.1, and predictions from all Ld layers are supervised. 

Efficiency. Our mapper and decoder use standard attention and MLP operations, so all attention layers run with FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022), whose memory scales linearly with sequence length for both self- and cross-attention. Compute is quadratic in N + K, but confined to the L = 4 

mapper layers, where runtime is largely bound by memory access rather than FLOPs. Following prior work (Chen et al., 2024), we cache past-frame features, so only the current frame is encoded. Dataset Splits. To rigorously evaluate gen-eralization and avoid geographic data leakage, we adopt the Near-Extrapolation split (Lilja et al., 2024) with a 60m ×30m perception range as our default benchmark for both nuScenes and Argoverse 2, following recent protocols (L¨ owens et al., 2025; Lyu et al., 2025). For completeness, we also report improvements on the original and StreamMapNet splits in Ap-pendix B.1. Limitations. (i) Replacing dense BEV features with compact 1D tokens may require additional adaptation in end-to-end frameworks, whose task decoders rely explicitly on the BEV grid structure (e.g., MapFormer and OccFormer in UniAD (Hu et al., 2023)). (ii) Our fixed token budget may require tuning for other tasks or datasets, and dynamic token selection (Rao et al., 2021; Fayyaz et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2022) is a promising direction for adapting the budget to input complexity.

Ablation on Token Count and Mapper Depth. Table 11 examines the effect of token count and mapper depth on accuracy. Increasing the number of tokens from 0.9k to 1.2k improves accuracy, but further increasing to 1.5k degrades it, possibly due to increasing redundancy among 1D map tokens. Varying the mapper depth shows a similar pattern: a depth of 3 is insufficient, while a depth of 5 slightly degrades accuracy, consistent with prior observations that transformers can exhibit diminishing returns as attention representations become increasingly redundant (Zhou et al., 2021). Our final configuration of 1.2k tokens and a mapper depth of 4 therefore offers the best accuracy among the settings tested, with FPS remaining nearly constant across all configurations. 

Ablation on Timesteps. Table 11 also shows that accuracy improves consistently up to 4 timesteps and slightly degrades at 6, making 4 timesteps the best-performing setting. This observation is con-sistent with MemFusionMap (Song et al., 2025), which conjectures that excessive temporal fusion dilutes the current-frame feature, and MambaMap (Yang et al., 2025b), which conjectures that an excessively long memory window introduces redundancy and noise. In real-world deployment, camera intrinsics are generally stable, while camera extrinsic parame-ters may be affected by calibration errors (Liao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). Following Map-TRv2 (Liao et al., 2025), we evaluate robustness to camera extrinsic perturbations by independently adding zero-mean Gaussian noise with varying standard deviations to the rotation and translation components of the camera extrinsics. Results are reported in Figure 8, where our method shows no degradation, maintaining 35.3 mAP on nuScenes and 63.3 mAP on Argoverse 2 across all per-turbation levels, as it does not explicitly rely on camera extrinsics. In contrast, under the largest perturbations, Dense BEV Projection drops from 26.7 mAP to 12.8 under rotation and 7.5 under translation on nuScenes, and from 53.1 mAP to 28.4 under rotation and 23.5 under translation on Argoverse 2.

For both online vectorized HD map construction and 3D object detection, we replace the original BEV projection modules with our self-attention mapper and use full instead of deformable cross-attention in the decoder. Unlike the single-frame MapTRv2 (Liao et al., 2025) setting, we use T = 4 

timesteps and 50 map tokens per camera per timestep, yielding 1.2k map tokens for nuScenes (6 cameras) and 1.4k for Argoverse 2 (7 cameras). F.1 ONLINE VECTORIZED HD M AP CONSTRUCTION 

We mainly follow the MapTRv2 (Liao et al., 2025) framework and training settings, with ImageNet-pretrained (Deng et al., 2009) image backbones and color-jitter augmentation. Input images are resized by a factor of 0.5 on nuScenes (1600 ×900 to 800 ×450) and, after padding to 2048 ×2048, by a factor of 0.3 on Argoverse 2. The decoder uses 50 instance queries, 20 point queries per element, and 6 layers, with classification, point, and direction loss weights of 2, 5, and 0.005. We remove MapTRv2’s depth, BEV segmentation, and PV segmentation auxiliary losses, since our mapper involves no depth estimation or BEV grid, and retain its one-to-many matching branch (300 extra instance queries, ground truth repeated 6 times). We train with AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017) with a weight decay of 0.01 and a batch size of 4 per GPU on 8 NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs for 24 epochs on nuScenes and 6 epochs on Argoverse 2, with an initial learning rate of 6 × 10 −4 and cosine decay. Adjacent input frames are 0.5 s apart on nuScenes and 0.4 s apart on Argoverse 2. F.2 3D O BJECT DETECTION 

We mainly follow the BEVFormer v2 (Yang et al., 2023) framework and training settings on nuScenes, including COCO-pretrained (Lin et al., 2014) backbone initialization and image-level data augmentation, with a ResNet-50 backbone and a 6-layer detection decoder. Unlike BEVFormer v2, which also fuses future frames and uses first-stage perspective proposals as hybrid object queries, we use only past frames (4-frame input, adjacent frames 0.5 s apart) and learned object queries.
