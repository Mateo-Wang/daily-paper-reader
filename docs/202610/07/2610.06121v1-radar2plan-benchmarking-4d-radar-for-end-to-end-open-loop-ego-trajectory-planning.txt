# Radar2Plan: Benchmarking 4D Radar for End-to-End Open-Loop Ego-Trajectory Planning 

Ling Yao 1,2, Yichun Xiao 1, Jin Jin 3, Yihan Zhang 2, Fangqiang Ding 1,∗

Abstract — Adverse weather and poor illumination remain major challenges for robust ego-trajectory planning in mobile autonomy. 4D radar offers reliable sensing under adverse conditions and direct radial-velocity measurements. However, sensing robustness does not necessarily translate into robust downstream planning, while existing 4D radar benchmarks focus primarily on perception rather than trajectory planning. We present Radar2Plan , a modular benchmark for open-loop ego-trajectory planning using real-world 4D radar data. 

Radar2Plan connects sensor encoders, scene representations, and planning heads through common interfaces, enabling controlled comparisons between different sensor and planner configurations. Using DSERT-RoLL and MAN TruckScenes, we evaluated seven combinations of camera, 4D radar, and LiDAR in four representative planning baselines and 12 weather and illumination conditions under a unified protocol. To our knowledge, Radar2Plan is the first benchmark dedicated to evaluating real-world 4D radars for autonomous driving plan-ning. Experiments show that 4D radar alone supports com-petitive ego-trajectory planning and robust performance under adverse conditions. Sensor-configuration comparisons further demonstrate its complementary value to other modalities, while revealing dependencies on sensor combination and planning architecture. The modular design also supports additional datasets, sensing modalities, and planners, providing a flexible foundation for future research on radar-based autonomous driving planning. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Mobile autonomous systems, including autonomous cars and trucks, UAVs, and construction machines, operate in safety-critical environments and must plan reliable motion from sensor observations. Cameras and LiDAR are widely used for environmental sensing, but cameras can be severely affected by poor illumination and adverse weather, while LiDAR measurements can also degrade in conditions such as rain, fog, and snow [11], [5], making robust motion planning challenging. In comparison, the 4D radar is less sensitive to these conditions and directly measures range, azimuth, elevation, and radial velocity [5], [7], making it a promising sensing modality for robust planning of autonomous driving. Fig. 1 illustrates the robustness of 4D radar sensing under adverse conditions against camera and LiDARs and its benefits to downstream trajectory planning. Despite these advantages, existing 4D radar studies have focused primarily on perception tasks such as object detec-tion, tracking, and occupancy prediction [5], [6], [7], leaving its value for downstream planning insufficiently evaluated. Supporting heterogeneous datasets, sensing modalities, and planning methods requires handling differences in data organization, sensor representations, and planner-specific interfaces. If treated independently for each experimental configuration, these differences can introduce implementation-specific variations and hinder controlled comparisons. To address this, Radar2Plan adopts a modular design that separates dataset adaptation, sensor input encod-ing, planning-specific scene representation, and the planning method through standardized interfaces. This design allows individual components to be exchanged independently while maintaining a consistent evaluation protocol across sensor configurations and planning methods. Radar2Plan evaluates seven combinations of camera, 4D radar, and LiDAR in four representative planning baselines: GRU-based waypoint regression adapted from TransFuser [15], sparse-query plan-ning adapted from SparseDrive [12], trajectory-vocabulary scoring adapted from VADv2 [16] and diffusion-based tra-jectory refinement adapted from DiffusionDrive [17]. Across both DSERT-RoLL and MAN TruckScenes, plan-ners using 4D radar as the sole sensing modality con-sistently outperform their camera and LiDAR-only coun-terparts. Adding 4D radar also generally improves multi-modal sensing-based planning, with particularly pronounced benefits under adverse weather and illumination conditions. These results demonstrate that the sensing robustness of 4D radar can translate into improved downstream ego-trajectory planning, and further highlight the importance of a dedicated benchmark for systematically evaluating this transfer across different sensor configurations, environmental conditions, and planning architectures. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:  

> •

We formulate and systematically investigate whether the sensing robustness of real-world 4D radar translates into improved open-loop ego-trajectory planning. To enable this study, we introduce Radar2Plan , which converts DSERT-RoLL and MAN TruckScenes into a unified planning benchmark with consistent task definitions, data splits and evaluation metrics.  

> •

We develop a modular design of the benchmark that enables controlled comparisons across datasets, sensing modalities, planner-specific scene representations, and planning methods, while allowing individual compo-nents to be changed or extended independently.  

> •

Through extensive evaluations across multiple sensor configurations and planning baselines, we show that 4D radar provides robust support for ego-trajectory plan-ning, particularly under adverse sensing conditions. The configuration-dependent results further highlight the im-portance of a dedicated benchmark for systematically assessing the planning value of 4D radar across different sensor combinations and planning architectures. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. 4D Radar Perception and Datasets 

Compared to conventional automotive radar, the 4D radar provides elevation-aware measurements and improved an-gular resolution while retaining robustness to poor illu-mination and adverse weather [11], [5]. These properties have motivated a growing number of 4D radar datasets and perception benchmarks. K-Radar [5], View-of-Delft [6], TJ4DRadSet [7], and DSERT-RoLL [8], for example, pri-marily evaluate 4D radar through tasks such as 3D object detection and multimodal perception. Radar-camera methods including CenterFusion [18], CRN [19], RCBEVDet [20], RaCFormer [21] and ZFusion [22] further demonstrate that radar features can complement visual observations for better 3D scene understanding. Collectively, these studies establish the sensing and perception value of 4D radar, but do not determine whether these advantages translate into improved downstream ego-trajectory planning. Evaluating this question requires real-world datasets that provide synchronized 4D radar together with other com-monly used sensing modalities and accurate ego-motion information. Among existing datasets, DSERT-RoLL [8] and MAN TruckScenes [9] satisfy these requirements by providing synchronized and calibrated camera, 4D-radar, LiDAR, and ego-motion measurements. B. End-to-End Ego-Trajectory Planning 

End-to-end autonomous driving methods predict future ego trajectories directly from sensor observations [23], [13], [14]. Existing approaches differ substantially in their scene representations and trajectory-generation mechanisms. TransFuser, TCP, and ThinkTwice employ direct waypoint or trajectory regression [15], [24], [25]. UniAD, VAD, and SparseDrive instead couple scene understanding and plan-ning through learned queries, vectorized representations, or sparse scene representations [13], [14], [12]. More recent methods explicitly model multiple possible ego trajectories: VADv2 scores candidates from a predefined trajectory vo-cabulary [16], GenAD learns a generative trajectory dis-tribution [26], and DiffusionDrive and GoalFlow generate multi-modal trajectories through truncated diffusion and flow matching, respectively [17], [27]. Despite this diversity in planning architectures, most ex-isting methods rely primarily on cameras, LiDAR, or their combination for environment sensing [15], [13], [14], [12]. SpaRC-AD [28] is a closely related exception that incorpo-rates conventional radar-camera fusion into a SparseDrive-based framework. However, it evaluates normal radar within a single planning architecture and does not study 4D radar. TruckDrive [10] provides real-world 4D radar data and a LiDAR-only UniAD ego-planning baseline. However, its re-leased planner does not use 4D radar as input. Consequently, existing works cannot establish whether the planning benefit of 4D radar is consistent across fundamentally different end-to-end trajectory-generation mechanisms. To distinguish modality-level effects to different plan-ning methods, we evaluate 4D radar across four representa-tive planning paradigms: direct waypoint regression, sparse-query planning, trajectory-vocabulary scoring, and diffusion-based trajectory refinement. We adopt TransFuser [15], SparseDrive [12], VADv2 [16], and DiffusionDrive [17] as representative methods for these four paradigms, re-spectively. This selection enables us to examine whether the observed planning value of 4D radar persists across substantially different scene representations and trajectory-generation mechanisms. As summarized in Table I, existing planning benchmarks and 4D radar datasets address complementary parts of the problem, motivating a unified benchmark to evaluate the planning value of 4D radar across sensor configurations, environmental conditions, and planning architectures. III. T HE Radar2Plan BENCHMARK 

As shown in Fig. 2, Radar2Plan uses dataset-specific adapters to convert continuous DSERT-RoLL and MAN TruckScenes recordings into a unified set of planning sam-ples, each defined at a reference timestamp and associated with a future ego trajectory. For each sensing modality, all planning baselines use the same encoder architecture to ensure consistent feature extraction. The encoded sensor features are then transformed into the scene representation required by each planning architecture. Finally, all planners produce scored candidate ego trajectories through a common output interface. 

A. Unified Multimodal Inputs 1) Datasets and Task Statistics: We use DSERT-RoLL [8] and MAN TruckScenes [9] to evaluate Radar2Plan across different road environments and vehicle platforms. DSERT-RoLL focuses on urban and suburban driving under six weather conditions. MAN TruckScenes covers autonomous-trucking scenarios on motorways, feeder and terminal roads. Together, they allow Radar2Plan to evaluate 4D radar-based trajectory planning in different road structures, weather conditions, sensing ranges, and ego-motion distributions. 2) Temporal Alignment and Trajectory Construction: The two datasets differ in file structure, sensor naming, sampling frequency, calibration format, and ego-pose representation. We therefore implement a dataset-specific adapter for each dataset. For each planning sample, the anchor time is the timestamp of the current sensor observations. Each adapter identifies valid anchor times, aligns the corresponding sensor observations with timestamped ego poses, and constructs a six-second future ego trajectory in the anchor ego frame. For DSERT-RoLL, synchronized sequence frames and their ego-pose records define candidate anchor times. Each target time is matched to the nearest future ego pose with a tolerance of 0.1 s . A sample is discarded if any of the 30 future poses cannot be matched. For MAN TruckScenes, the official 2Hz keyframes define the anchors. Future ego poses are linearly interpolated at 5 Hz , while sensor observations are associated through the corresponding keyframe token. B. Sensor Encoders 

The sensor encoders transform raw observations into fea-ture representations with a common hidden dimension. We keep the encoder architecture for each sensing modality fixed across all four planning baselines, thereby minimizing confounding effects from differences in encoders of different sensors and enabling a more controlled comparison of the planning architectures. 

1) Camera Encoder: We use DINOv3 as the common camera encoder [29]. It provides pretrained dense visual features for downstream tasks. All formal configurations use a frozen DINOv3 ViT-S/16 backbone with the same image preprocessing and an input resolution of 192 × 288 . Only the output projection is trained in each configuration. 

2) Radar and LiDAR Encoders: We use separate PTv3-based encoders for 4D radar and LiDAR [30]. Both encoders consist of three PTv3 layers with a patch size of 64 and a grid resolution of 0.5 m . The two modalities use separate input projections and independently parameterized encoders to account for their different point-wise attributes. Before temporal aggregation, each radar point is repre-sented by its ego-frame position (x, y, z ), reflectivity, and a Doppler-derived velocity attribute. For DSERT-RoLL, this velocity attribute corresponds to the provided Doppler mea-surement, whereas for MAN TruckScenes, it is given by the longitudinal relative velocity expressed in the vehicle’s ego-centric frame. Each LiDAR point is represented by its position (x, y, z ), intensity, and relative scan time. For both radar and LiDAR, the input is formed by aggre-gating the current point cloud with the preceding frame. The preceding point cloud is motion-compensated into the current ego frame, and a frame-level temporal offset is appended to each point. After temporal aggregation, we retain at most 2,048 radar points and 4,096 LiDAR points per sample. The radar and LiDAR encoders are initialized from scratch for each training run. Although their architectures are fixed across experiments, their parameters are learned indepen-dently for each sensor and planning baseline configuration.

D. Controlled Planning Baselines 

Using the scene representations described above, 

Radar2Plan evaluates four representative trajectory-generation methods under a unified output interface. To support matched sensor comparisons, we adapt the representative planning components of each baseline to the common benchmark task. Panel D of Fig. 2 summarizes their respective planning heads and trajectory outputs. 

1) TransFuser-Style Waypoint Regression: The TransFuser-style planner uses the scene representation and ego state to initialize a gated recurrent unit (GRU) [15]. The GRU sequentially predicts 30 two-dimensional waypoint increments, which are accumulated to obtain the future ego trajectory in the current ego frame. Since this planner produces a single trajectory, we set KGRU = 1 .

2) SparseDrive-Style Sparse-Query Planning: The SparseDrive-style planner represents the scene using 64 learned agent queries and 32 learned map queries [12]. A planning query interacts with these scene queries, after which the planning head generates three candidate ego trajectories and their mode scores, yielding KSQ = 3 .

3) VADv2-Style Trajectory-Vocabulary Scoring: The VADv2-style planner constructs 64 fixed trajectory proto-types using only the training set, with the closest prototype serving as the supervision target [16]. At inference, the planner scores the prototypes and selects the highest-scoring trajectory candidates without residual refinement, yielding 

KVocab = 64 .

4) DiffusionDrive-Style Trajectory Refinement: The DiffusionDrive-style planner initializes 64 trajectory anchors from a vocabulary constructed exclusively from the training set [17]. Conditioned on the scene representation, these anchors are refined through a deterministic two-step truncated-diffusion process with fixed noise. A separate scene-conditioned scoring head assigns a mode score to each refined trajectory, yielding KDiff = 64 .

A. Training Protocol and Implementation 

We train every configuration for 80 epochs using AdamW. We set the learning rate to 10 −4, the weight decay to 10 −4,and the batch size to 16. All runs use seed 42 without a learning-rate scheduler. Camera inputs are resized to 192 × 288 . Each sample retains at most 2048 radar points and 4096 LiDAR points. The camera branch uses the current synchronized images. The radar and LiDAR branches combine the current point cloud with one preceding frame. The models receive a six-step ego-motion history sampled at 5 Hz. Each 11-D history token encodes relative planar displacement, relative yaw, its sine and cosine, speed, and yaw rate. It also includes longitudinal and lateral acceleration, curvature, and age. The current ego-state vector contains speed, yaw, global x/y 

position, yaw rate, and three zero-padded entries. Each planning baseline retains its baseline-specific training objective. The TransFuser-style GRU baseline uses an L1 loss on waypoint positions. The SparseDrive-style baseline combines a winner-mode increment L1 loss with a 0.5-weighted sigmoid focal loss for mode scoring. The VADv2-style baseline uses cross-entropy over the nearest fixed trajectory prototype. The DiffusionDrive-style baseline com-bines scene-scoring cross-entropy with an L1 trajectory loss weighted by 8. We clip its trajectory residuals at 5 m and exclude targets whose six-second endpoint exceeds 200 m. Each baseline uses one checkpoint-selection rule across all sensor configurations. The GRU, sparse-query, and vocabu-lary baselines select the checkpoint with the lowest baseline-specific validation loss. The diffusion baseline selects the checkpoint with the lowest internal-development Top1ADE after its mode cross-entropy passes the configured gate.

D. Discussion 

The experimental results reveal two complementary roles of 4D radar in ego-trajectory planning. As the only sensing modality, the 4D radar enables strong and consistent planning performance across different driving domains and trajectory-generation mechanisms. When combined with other sensing modalities, its contribution depends on how effectively the planning architecture integrates heterogeneous sensor fea-tures. The particularly large improvements under adverse weather and challenging illumination indicate that 4D radar is especially valuable when camera or LiDAR observations become less reliable. At the same time, the two DSERT-RoLL cases in which adding radar increases Top1ADE show that introducing radar features alone does not guarantee improved planning performance. V. C ONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS 

We introduced Radar2Plan , a modular benchmark for evaluating real-world 4D radar in open-loop ego-trajectory planning. Across DSERT-RoLL and MAN TruckScenes, 4D radar provides strong planning performance as a sole sensing modality and generally improves multi-modal planning, with the largest benefits under adverse weather and illumina-tion. These results show that the sensing robustness of 4D radar can translate into improved downstream planning and motivate controlled evaluation across sensor configurations and planning architectures. The current evaluation is limited to two road-vehicle datasets and open-loop planning on recorded sequences.
