## ABSTRACT 

Recent video world models have witnessed the paradigm shift from single-agent to multi-agent involvements, which can reveal more complicated dynamics and cross-agent interaction in the real world. However, existing approaches com-monly adopt implicit inter-agent communications via cross attention, which lack explicit geometry constraints and unified 3D state, thereby leading to poor multi-view consistency and struggling with recovering out-of-sight agents. In addition, most of them assume a static background, failing to represent uncontrolled back-ground dynamics. To address these problems, we propose Artemis: a geometry-grounded multi-agent world model with explicit memory sharing. An explicit 3D world map is reconstructed from multi-agent observations to enforce a uni-fied 3D state across agents, offering high cross-view consistency. Specifically, an action-guided geometric injection module is developed to simultaneously ren-der decomposed foreground-background control maps, which are then injected

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Video world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Po et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025) can predict future environmental dynamics given historical observations and ac-tions from users or agents, which have recently achieved remarkable success in autonomous driving (Wang et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026d;c), embodied AI (Lu et al., 2025; Seo et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2026b), and immersive game engines (Yu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). How-ever, early methods are mostly constrained by a single-agent perspective, which cannot reflect the intrinsic multi-agent interactions and complex dynamics in the real world. To effectively extend to multi-agent interactive world models, some recent approaches attempt to achieve inter-agent information exchange in a shared world. As a pioneering work, Solaris (Savva et al., 2026) curates a novel multiplayer dataset in gaming scenes and introduces shared self-attention layers for cross-agent correlation. Subsequent methods further improve it with scalability in agent numbers (Liu et al., 2026a; Wu et al., 2026), accurate agent positional encoding (Sun et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026a), and world state alignment (Wu et al., 2026; Hu et al., 2026). However, these multi-agent approaches commonly struggle with three challenges: (1) Lack of ex-plicit geometric constraint for cross-view consistency : The physical environment and action-triggered events should evolve consistently and be faithfully reflected from all agents’ perspectives. This requires a globally consistent understanding of geometric structures and visual appearances across all agents. MultiWorld (Wu et al., 2026) proposes an action attention and reweighting module to maintain a global state. However, their inter-agent association is still established on implicit cross attention, lacking sufficient geometric cues to ensure multi-view consistency. (2) Limited memory sharing : Basically, diverse agents execute their actions and exist in the same shared world, which means that historical observed frames across agents may share high overlap, especially in scenarios when another agent revisits the same location. Therefore, sufficient historical memory sharing is crucial to further strengthen a unified world state and facilitate cross-agent information reuse. How-ever, existing methods (Savva et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026; Hu et al., 2026) mostly fail to consider effective cross-agent geometry sharing beyond the current timestamp. (3) Over-simplified scene complexity and dynamics : Previous methods typically evaluate the multi-agent interaction in the synthetic gameplay scenario where dynamics are quite simplified to reveal the true world evolution, leading to uncertain generalization capabilities in scaling agent complexity, memorizing out-of-sight dynamics, and handling severe occlusions in complex world distributions. To address these bottlenecks, we propose Artemis, a geometry-grounded multi-agent world model with shared explicit 3D state and memory. Rather than implicit-only agent association, our method constructs a 3D global point cloud-based world map with cross-view consistency to address the first challenge. Explicit geometric constraints are then rendered as control maps and then injected into Diffusion Transformer (DiT) through GeoAdapter, enabling global-aware and view-invariant video generation for each agent. Specifically, we decompose the scene into static background, action-controlled foreground agents, and uncontrolled background dynamics. Static geometry and con-trolled agents’ proxies provide background and foreground RGB and depth control maps, respec-2Preprint tively, while uncontrolled non-agent dynamics further require multi-frame historical control maps providing consistent motion tendency for future synthesis. For the second bottleneck, we further introduce two novel designs to facilitate effective cross-agent memory sharing: (1) World map sharing. We progressively select keyframes revealing significantly unobserved areas from new generated videos across all agents to update a global world map. In this case, different agents can explicit share a consistent 3D world state with multi-view matching priors. (2) Relative pose sharing. During autoregressive rollout, persistent agent proxies are prop-agated using cross-agent relative poses and updated from rolling-out videos. Another benefit of maintaining relative pose is that, when an unrepresented controlled agent is expected to enter the view, a pose-dependent visibility test can initialize its proxy. To address the third challenge, we curate a novel dataset from the CARLA simulator in complex urban scenarios called MA-CARLA, which provides scalable multi-camera sampling, abundant interaction modes, physical plausibility, and diverse environmental dynamics. Overall, the key contributions of our paper are as follows: • We propose a novel multi-agent world model called Artemis with strong explicit geometric constraints and cross-agent memory sharing. Compared to prior methods that only leverage implicit feature exchange, our method recovers and dynamically maintains synchronized 3D world states, offering superior cross-view consistency. • Hybrid geometry-pose memory sharing provides complementary memories for disentan-gled statics and dynamics. An explicit world map memory is designed by progressively reconstructing static background from new generated videos which are integrated across agents. Additionally, relative-pose visibility checks can support proxy initialization of sud-denly emerging vehicles. • A novel dataset is designed based on the CARLA simulator, which enables physical plau-sibility, diverse inter-agent interaction modes, multi-camera sampling, and complicated environmental dynamics. • Extensive experiments demonstrate that our Artemis surpasses prior methods in terms of vi-sual fidelity, cross-view consistency, and uncontrolled background dynamics. Notably, our Artemis can support both single-camera and multi-camera setting, and also simultaneously roll out multi-modal driving scenes with consistent 2D and 3D representations. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Interactive Video World Models. World models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018) can predict future environmental dynamics given historical observations and actions. With rapid advances in recent video diffusion transformers, there is increasing research interest resorting to action-conditioned video generation backbones (Wan et al., 2025) for world modeling. Recently, interactivity has be-come a crucial property in world models where users can immersively navigate, manipulate, and personalize their created worlds in a multi-round paradigm. As a milestone, Genie (Bruce et al., 2024) compresses the video features into latent space with VAE and then learn actions unsupervis-edly, supporting minute-level video generation and interactive exploration. Subsequent world mod-els focus on achieving long-horizon consistency (Po et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2026a; Wang et al., 2026), effective memory design (Wu et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2025; Hong et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026a), or real-time rollout (Chen et al., 2026c; Huang et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2026a). However, most of video world models enforce a single-agent formulation with a first-person or third-person perspective, with limited agent numbers and exploration flexibility (Liu et al., 2026b). 

Multi-Agent Video World Models. To capture inter-instance interactions, MultiVerse (team, 2025) proposes the first multiplayer gaming world model by trivially concatenating each agent’s video and action tokens along the channel dimension for denoising together. Solaris (Savva et al., 2026) further designs a shared self-attention and per-player ID embeddings. Nevertheless, its per-slot embeddings break the permutation-symmetry requirement for each agent. Moreover, Solaris suffers from poor generalization to arbitrary agent numbers due to quadratic-complexity full attention. To address these problems, Gamma-World (Liu et al., 2026a) introduces a regular simplex in rotary angle space to achieve equidistant agent embeddings and sparse hub attention to decrease computational costs. We preprocess synchronized CARLA videos into geometry-aware conditioning signals. For each agent–camera streaming sequence, LingBot-Map (Chen et al., 2026b) is used to estimates dense depth, intrinsics, and camera poses in a consistent local coordinate system. The same estimates are used for point lifting, proxy fitting, and control rendering to preserve the non-metric scale. Grounded-SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024) extracts candidate dynamic masks, associated with either action-controlled agents or uncontrolled actors using CARLA metadata and calibrated projections. We exclude both categories from static-map fusion. Controlled agents are fitted as full-covariance 3D Gaussian proxies, while uncontrolled actors are stored as time-stamped observations rather than assigned future trajectories as in Fig. 3. Artemis then renders a unified 24-channel con-trol tensor consisting of background RGB/depth, proxy RGB/depth, a synthesis mask, uncontrolled-history RGB/depth, and an age mask within a certain historical window.

Previous multi-agent methods (Sun et al., 2026; Savva et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026; Hu et al., 2026) use primarily 2D-based world representations, suffering from degraded cross-camera and cross-agent consistency and lacking 3D geometric guidance. To tackle these problems, we reconstruct and progressively update a sparse point cloud Pt as a shared 3D state for all agents. Overall Pipeline. As in Fig. 4, we first initialize a shared static world map from the agents’ initial observations with LingBot-Map (Chen et al., 2026b), a feed-forward foundation model for streaming 3D reconstruction, where submaps from all agents are registered in a common coordinate system. Given the input actions, we derive agent trajectories and render foreground-background disentangled control maps from the reconstructed world (Yang et al., 2026). Specifically, controlled-agent proxies fitted from the first frames are propagated along these trajectories, with visibility-triggered agent insertion when needed. We render the static map, controlled proxies, and available uncontrolled dynamics observation history into a unified set of controls for the planned camera views, encoding both geometry and historical dynamics. Afterwards, a GeoAdapter module injects these controls into a pre-trained video diffusion transformer, Wan2.2 5B (Wan et al., 2025), which jointly generates synchronized future frames for all agents. Another cross attention layer (Savva et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026) is also designed to communicate inter-agent information in latent space. After generation, reconstructed static content updates the shared map, and accepted uncontrolled dynamics update their 3D history. Clean generated latents also update the implicit per-agent historical memories in KV-Cache for autoregressive online rollout. 4.2 SHARED 3D W ORLD STATE CONSTRUCTION

Static Map Update. After each rollout, we will select a few keyframes from newly generated frames in the block to update the unified 3D world map. The chosen keyframes are the frames introducing un-seen scenarios or sharing low overlap with historical observations. The final world map is updated by merging the original static geometry map and the newly recovered submap reconstructed from keyframes. 

Uncontrolled Dynamic Buffer Maintenance. For each uncontrolled segmented dynamics, we update the history 3D buffer using a first-in-first-out principle, discarding the farthest frames while dynamically retaining the latest frame. In this case, the future positions can be extrapolated from the progressively updated historical motion priors. 

Structured Pose Memory Maintenance. For each controllable agent, we maintain their historical poses across rollout blocks, so that an agent can remain geometrically consistent even when it is temporarily occluded or outside the observer’s sight. If one agent is not visible in the current frame, the generation model cannot generate it in the following block as original control maps fail to aware its existence through current frame segmentation. To address this issue, we design a pose-guided verification module based on maintained pose memory as in Fig. 5. If no proxy is available is the current frame but the target’s planned poses indicate potential entry into the observer’s field of view, we initialize a conservative vehicle-shaped Gaussian and integrate it with the target position. This procedure allows the model to possess a global awareness of each agent’s location and feasibly supports the generation of newly visible controlled agents at any timestamp.

Comparison Baselines. Since recent multi-agent world models are mostly still close-sourced, we choose a representative method Solaris (Savva et al., 2026), using implicit shared self-attention for cross-agent association. We also migrate a similar strategy as in OASIS (Decart et al., 2024) by direct concatenating multi-agent tokens along feature channels. 

Evaluation Metrics. We report eight metrics covering both appearance fidelity and multi-agent consistency. For appearance metrics, we compute PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS between each gener-ated video and its ground-truth video. For multi-agent consistency, we use DA3 (Lin et al., 2025) to process each generated video and estimate depth, camera intrinsics, and camera poses. Sub-ject Consistency and Background Consistency evaluate the temporal stability of subject appearance and background structure, respectively. We also report the Aesthetic Quality and Imaging Quality, reflecting frame-level aesthetic appeal and overall perceptual quality, respectively. 

Comparison Results. As in Table 1, our Artemis outperforms the baseline method Solaris and OASIS by a large margin in both appearance-related metrics and also consistency. Compared to Solaris, our Artemis achieves almost double SSIM, while reducing LPIPS to almost half. This proves that our rendered videos possess higher generation quality with an explicit geometry-guided paradigm and powerful generation priors from DiT. As for multi-agent consistency, our method also significantly surpasses Solaris in both cross-view consistency and trajectory consistency. This is mainly because implicit-only methods cannot recover underlying 3D world states, suffering from severe camera pose changes, occlusions, or few-shot views. 5.3 QUALITATIVE RESULTS

For scalability beyond two agents, we extend the pairwise pipeline by treating each vehicle as an observer and all other vehicles as controlled targets. Unlike the two-agent case, where the visible foreground object can usually be assigned to the other agent directly, the multi-agent setting requires identity-aware target matching because each observer may see multiple vehicles. For each observer, detected vehicle masks are matched to the other agents using projected trajectories, proxy-track predictions, depth consistency, and image-space distance. The matched targets are then fitted as proxy Gaussians, rolled forward with their own trajectories, and merged into one dynamic control map for that observer. We conduct ablation studies to analyze the contribution of the geometric conditioning, online world update, and temporal memory mechanisms. The results are summarized in Table 2. First, remov-ing depth-related conditions consistently degrades reconstruction quality. This confirms that the dynamic proxy depth provide complementary geometric guidance. Second, disabling online world map update also hurts performance, reducing background and subject consistencies. This indicates that maintaining and updating the world representation during rollout is important for long-horizon consistency. Without map update, accumulated generation errors cannot be corrected by newly ob-served content, leading to weaker view reconstruction quality. Reducing the KV-cache with fewer 9Preprint historical frames also leads to worse perceptual quality and consistency. The result suggests that the causal KV-cache is important for preserving coherent autoregressive history. 

## 6 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we propose a novel multi-agent world model which is anchored by reconstructed 3D world state. To incorporate large-scale dynamics, we curate a new driving dataset based on the CARLA simulator. Unlike previous methods mostly leveraging implicit cross-agent correlation, our Artemis can explicitly reconstruct and dynamically update a synchronized 3D world map. We also design a history 3D buffer for uncontrolled environmental dynamics, leveraging historical obser-vations to predict their future motion. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed novelty, spatio-temporal consistency across frames, and scalability to more agent numbers.

In preprocessing, each agent’s observation stream is reconstructed with LingBot-Map, which esti-mates dense depth, camera intrinsics, and camera poses in a consistent local coordinate gauge. We 13 Preprint use these estimates jointly for background point lifting, controllable-proxy fitting, ambient-history lifting, and control-map rendering. CARLA annotations are used for identity association and mo-tion supervision, but any metric quantities used by the renderer are converted into the LingBot-Map gauge before being combined with reconstructed geometry. During online rollout, the initial observations define the reference scale and camera rig geometry. LingBot-Map reconstructs the initial local scene and provides the intrinsics and camera poses used for subsequent rendering and ray-map construction. User actions, inter-agent offsets, proxy extents, and generated-frame reconstructions are expressed in this same reference gauge before map fusion or dynamic-history insertion. Thus, Artemis maintains a single consistent geometric scale throughout autoregressive rollout, without requiring per-block scale re-estimation. A.2 THREE -S TAGE TRAINING PROCEDURE 

We design a three-stage training recipe using Diffusion-Forcing (Chen et al., 2024) and Self-Forcing (Huang et al., 2025) and tailor them for multi-agent online rollout: 

Stage 1: Bidirectional Teacher for Geometry Grounding. We first train a bidirectional teacher network to guarantee high-quality geometry reconstruction and effective control map injection. This stage jointly trains the cross-agent attention layer, GeoAdapter, and DiT on full sequences for all agents, using pre-processed control maps. Stage 1 can exploit larger temporal receptive field, cross-agent interactions, and spatio-temporal consistency, which serves as a real teacher for stage 3 distil-lation. 

Stage 2: Causal Student with Diffusion-Forcing. Stage 2 training is initialized based on the pretrained checkpoint form stage 1. However, we switch the temporally non-causal training in stage 1 to autoregressive training with causal masks using Diffusion-Forcing (Chen et al., 2024). Variant noise scales are implemented on different video frames in DiT, while the noise scales for various agents are also independently sampled. Notably, the generation process is still established on full frame sequences in a single forward pass. 

Stage 3: Self-Forcing-based Distillation. For stage 3, we leverage online streaming rollout, au-toregressively generating videos based on history self-rollouts in a chunk-wise paradigm (Huang et al., 2025), as in Sec. 4.1. This step reduces the diffusion denoising process into fewer steps which improves online inference efficiency. Specifically, control maps are first online rendered from the initialized 3D reconstructed world and intermediate actions. Then, latent frames are generated causally, where each agent will maintain an independent KV Cache to store its own historical latent frames as memory. Finally, the generated chunk is decoded into current videos, which are then used to reconstruct the updated 3D world map. We instantiate three models with identical architectures but distinct roles: a causal generator initialized from stage 2, a frozen bidirectional real teacher initialized from stage 1, and a trainable bidirectional fake critic, also initialized from stage 1. The real teacher represents the data distribution learned from the real videos, whereas the fake critic continuously tracks the evolving distribution induced by the causal generator.
