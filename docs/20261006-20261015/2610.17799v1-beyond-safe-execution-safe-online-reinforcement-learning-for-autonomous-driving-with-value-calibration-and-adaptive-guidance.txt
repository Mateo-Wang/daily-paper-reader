Beyond Safe Execution: Safe Online Reinforcement Learning for
Autonomous Driving with Value Calibration and Adaptive Guidance
Zeqiao Li, Yijing Wang, Haoyu Wang, Zheng Li, and Zhiqiang Zuo
Abstract— Online reinforcement learning (RL) enables driv-
ing policies to adapt to changing deployment conditions, but
requires safe exploration throughout training. Safety filtering
alone does not ensure stable policy improvement or reduced de-
pendence on corrections. We propose Safe Online Adaptive Re-
finement (SOAR-Flow), a framework for safe and stable online
fine-tuning of offline-initialized Flow-based driving policies. A
control barrier function (CBF)–model predictive control (MPC)
filter corrects potentially unsafe actions before execution. To
support stable policy updates, we introduce Gaussian-return-
aware value calibration based on calibrated Q-learning (Cal-
QL), using recorded behavior returns and predicted return vari-
ability to smooth conservative critic updates and mitigate policy
degradation. Paired corrective learning teaches feasible action
adjustments, while an intervention-to-go predictor encourages
decisions requiring fewer future corrections. Its predictions
also regulate guidance strength, allowing recovery supervision
to weaken as predicted dependence decreases. Together with
gradually reduced calibration regularization, this supports
continued task improvement while preserving hard execution
constraints. We analyze conditions for safe execution at control
steps and diminishing auxiliary learning influences. We evaluate
SOAR-Flow in simulation and on an uncrewed ground vehicle
(UGV). The results demonstrate improved driving performance
and reduced reliance on safety corrections during protected on-
line fine-tuning. The demonstration video and project overview
are available at https://lzqw.github.io/SOAR-Flow/.
I. INTRODUCTION
Learning safe and efficient driving behavior in changing
environments remains a central challenge in autonomous
driving [1]. Online RL enables continued policy improve-
ment through interaction, with offline demonstrations provid-
ing useful initial skills [2], [3]. On UGVs, however, trial-and-
error exploration can lead to collisions and other accidents,
making safety essential throughout online learning [4].
Preventing failures before they occur is a practical ap-
proach to safe exploration. Failure-Aware Offline-to-Online
Reinforcement Learning (FARL) follows this principle
through failure prediction and preventive recovery in robotic
manipulation [5]. Model-based safety filters check actions
against explicit constraints and compute feasible corrections
before execution [6]. However, successful protected execu-
tion can still depend on repeated corrections, while online
updates may degrade previously learned behavior [2], [6].
Going beyond safe execution therefore requires learning
effectively from protected experience to improve task per-
formance and reduce correction demand.
Reliable value estimates are central to this improvement.
Overestimation may favor actions with limited data support,
whereas excessive pessimism can undervalue established
behaviors as new experience is incorporated [2]. Filtering
also changes the executed action, so observed rewards and
transitions need to be associated with the action actually
applied [6]. Cal-QL addresses value-scale mismatch by cali-
brating conservative estimates against a reference behavior’s
return [2]. This motivates calibration that uses recorded
execution outcomes while accounting for return variability
during continued online learning.
Learning from corrected actions can reduce repeated in-
terventions [6]. Human–AI Copilot Optimization (HACO)
estimates cumulative human intervention costs to guide
policy optimization [7], motivating consideration of future
assistance alongside immediate corrections. In driving, ear-
lier position or speed choices can determine whether later
corrections are needed. Moreover, optimizing a finite-horizon
MPC cost need not maximize long-horizon task return.
Corrective guidance should therefore combine feasible local
adjustments with anticipation of future interventions, while
allowing its influence to diminish as dependence decreases.
This leaves room for continued reward-driven improvement
under unchanged safety constraints.
To address these challenges, we propose SOAR-Flow, a
framework for safe online refinement of offline-initialized
Flow-based driving policies. CBF–MPC certifies references
before execution to protect online interaction. We introduce
Gaussian-return-aware value calibration by extending Cal-
QL with the Gaussian return model of distributional soft
actor–critic (DSAC) [2], [8]. Recorded behavior returns
provide calibration references, while predicted return vari-
ability smooths conservative critic updates. Paired correc-
tive learning teaches feasible action adjustments, and an
intervention-to-go predictor encourages earlier adjustments
to reduce subsequent corrections. The same predictions reg-
ulate guidance strength, allowing recovery supervision to
weaken as predicted dependence decreases. Together with
decaying calibration regularization, this supports continued
task improvement under unchanged execution constraints.
The main contributions are as follows:
• A Gaussian-return-aware extension of Cal-QL combines
recorded behavior returns with predicted return variabil-
ity to smooth conservative critic updates and support
stable online learning from protected execution.
• An adaptive guidance mechanism combines paired cor-
rective learning with intervention-to-go prediction. The
predictor guides policy actions and regulates guidance
strength to reduce filter reliance while retaining task
optimization.
• We establish conditions for sample-time safety of pro-

II. RELATED WORK
Safe RL and intervention-based learning. Safe RL
addresses safety through policy optimization and execution-
time protection. Constrained policy optimization regulates
expected costs in policy updates [9], whereas recovery
policies and predictive safety filters intervene using learned
risk estimates or model-based feasibility checks [10], [11].
For pretrained policies, SafeFlow enforces barrier conditions
during test-time flow-based trajectory generation [12], while
FARL uses an offline-trained world model and recovery
policy to prevent failures during online refinement, keep-
ing both protection components fixed online [5]. Beyond
execution protection, interventions also provide information
for policy learning. Safety Filtering While Training studies
how filtering and correction penalties affect learned behav-
ior [6], while CBF-RL combines training-time filtering with
barrier-inspired rewards to encourage safer actions without
runtime correction [13]. HACO estimates cumulative human
intervention costs to guide policy optimization [7]. Building
on this connection between protection and learning, SOAR-
Flow combines paired corrective supervision with prediction
of future filter use. The predictions guide policy actions and
regulate supervision strength, while CBF–MPC certification
remains active.
Value learning and online policy refinement. Online
refinement seeks to improve from new experience while
retaining useful pretrained behavior. Offline RL addresses
unsupported actions through conservative value estimation
in conservative Q-learning or in-dataset value learning in
implicit Q-learning [14], [15]. However, excessive pessimism
can hinder the transition to online learning, motivating Cal-
QL’s calibration against a reference behavior’s return [2].
Policy regularization and objective alignment offer com-
plementary approaches: PROTO evolves its policy regular-
izer during fine-tuning [16], while Uni-O4 uses a shared
on-policy objective across offline and online stages [17].
Generative policies complement these optimization methods
by representing diverse behaviors in prior data. Flow Q-
learning combines flow-based action modeling with value-
driven training of an expressive one-step actor [3]; RL-100
connects imitation, iterative offline RL, and online refinement
for diffusion-based robotic policies [18]. Building on Cal-
QL and DSAC’s Gaussian return model [2], [8], SOAR-
Flow uses predicted return variability to smooth behavior-
referenced calibration of values learned from certified exe-
cution. This value-learning objective complements adaptive
intervention guidance during protected online policy refine-
ment.

sequences guide policy improvement. Adaptive guidance re-
duces auxiliary learning influences as predicted filter depen-
dence decreases. Fig. 1 summarizes this protected interaction
and update loop.

IV. SIMULATION EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate whether SOAR-Flow improves an initialized
driving policy while limiting training violations and reducing
reliance on safety corrections across different simulated
scenarios. Baseline comparisons and ablations examine the
contributions of value calibration and adaptive guidance.
A. Experimental Setup
We conduct experiments in SafeMetaDriveEnv of
MetaDrive [23], where scenario seeds specify different road
layouts and obstacle configurations. Fig. 2 shows a sce-
nario and the experimental workstation. An MPC controller
collects offline trajectories on ten fixed scenario seeds to
initialize the Flow policy and Gaussian task critics. Twenty
additional seeds, excluded from offline training, are used for
online fine-tuning and evaluation. The actor receives a 259-
dimensional observation comprising 19 ego-state and naviga-
tion variables, including speed, heading, steering state, road-
boundary distances, and upcoming checkpoints, together
with 240 light detection and ranging (LiDAR)-like range
measurements. Four additional safety-context variables are
provided only to the task critics. The normalized actor output

Comparisons and metrics. Baselines include fixed CBF–

using ιt. Repeated trials are averaged within each training
seed, followed by the mean and sample SD across seeds.

338.0 and 348.0, respectively. Mean-only calibration narrows
the return gap, but its listed correction rate remains higher at
1.20%. The full-method entry combines the highest return
with the lowest training cost among these variants. PO
safe-success entries are also lower without correction or P,
linking the tabulated comparison to task completion as well
as correction demand.
V. REAL-WORLD EXPERIMENTS
We examine protected online adaptation on a UGV and
the development of driving behavior requiring fewer safety
corrections. A frozen-policy test on a held-out cornering task
further assesses execution beyond the training layouts.
Fig. 4: UGV experimental platform.
A. Experimental Setup
Our UGV in Fig. 4 is equipped with LiDAR, cameras,
radar, and an integrated navigation system (INS). Policy
inputs combine ego motion, surrounding obstacle distances,
and navigation checkpoints [4]. Policy inference and NMPC
run onboard at 10 Hz and 20 Hz, respectively. The actor
specifies a local reference; following certification, NMPC
generates steering and longitudinal commands transmitted
through the base adapter unit (BAU).
Offline data are collected by a human driver in the two-
sided obstacle-bypass layout of Fig. 5(a). We record 24
demonstration trajectories, with 12 passing on each side,
to pretrain the UGV Flow policy and Gaussian critics.
Online refinement alternates between Blocked-Side Bypass in
Fig. 5(b), where one demonstrated passing route is blocked,
and 8-shaped Maneuver in Fig. 5(c), which requires suc-
cessive turns in opposite directions around two obstacles.
The first task changes which demonstrated bypass remains
feasible; the second requires the exit from one bypass to
accommodate the following turn. A single policy is updated
across both tasks under CBF–MPC protection, with their ex-
perience stored in the same online replay buffer. Checkpoints

B. Results and Analysis
Online adaptation. Fig. 5(e–f) and Table II show blocked-
side adaptation. SOAR-Flow achieves 100% SS and a return
of 350.8 ± 1.8, compared with 343.9 ± 2.6 for Gaussian-
DSAC. Its correction rate is 0.68 ± 0.11%, versus 2.46 ±
0.32% for Gaussian-DSAC and 4.31 ± 0.41% for Flow-BC.
The 39.6 return gain over Flow-BC accompanies reduced
correction demand after one demonstrated route is blocked.
Autonomous execution and efficiency. Fig. 6 shows 8-
shaped refinement from corrected early and intermediate
runs to late execution without CBF correction, with nominal
MPC tracking retained. The first turn must prepare for
the following opposite-direction turn. In Table II, SOAR-
Flow reaches 100% 8-shaped SS, compared with 95% for
CBF–MPC and 90% for Gaussian-DSAC. Its completion
time is 15.36 ± 0.24 s, versus 16.72 ± 0.31 s for CBF–
MPC, a reduction of 1.36 s (approximately 8.1%). The
corresponding minimum clearances are 0.801±0.019 m and
0.779±0.018 m. Thus, shorter completion time accompanies
slightly larger mean clearance.
Zero-shot cornering. On held-out cornering, SOAR-Flow
attains 95% SS, compared with 85% for fixed CBF–MPC,
55% for Flow-BC, and 90% for Gaussian-DSAC. Fig. 5(d)
contrasts SOAR-Flow clearing both obstacles with fixed
CBF–MPC contacting the downstream obstacle, highlighting
the need to preserve clearance for subsequent avoidance. The
frozen policy transfers without further updates, although not
every evaluation succeeds.
VI. CONCLUSION
This paper presented SOAR-Flow for safe online refine-
ment of offline-initialized Flow-based driving policies un-
der CBF–MPC protection. Gaussian-return-aware calibration
uses recorded behavioral outcomes to regulate critic updates,
while paired corrections and intervention-to-go learning
guide policy improvement. Intervention-responsive guidance
and decaying calibration weights reduce auxiliary influences
under the stated conditions. The analysis establishes condi-
tional sample-time safety of protected execution and vanish-
ing auxiliary gradients. Together, these components connect
protected data collection with continued task optimization,
aiming to reduce reliance on particular recovery behaviors
while retaining the prescribed execution constraints.
