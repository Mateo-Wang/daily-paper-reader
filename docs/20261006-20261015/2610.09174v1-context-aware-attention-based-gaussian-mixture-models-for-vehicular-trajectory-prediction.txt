Context-aware Attention-based Gaussian Mixture
Models for Vehicular Trajectory Prediction

Abstract—Reliable and interpretable trajectory prediction is
critical for cooperative and autonomous driving in complex
and uncertain environments. This paper introduces a Context-
Aware Attention-based Gaussian Mixture Model (CAA-GMM)
for multimodal, uncertainty-aware motion forecasting. The pro-
posed approach models future motion as a probabilistic mixture
conditioned on both scene context and agent dynamics, capturing
diverse behavioral modes with interpretable Gaussian compo-
nents. A lightweight attention mechanism adaptively encodes
inter-agent interactions and contextual salience, enabling efficient
fusion of rasterized environment cues and motion history in
dense traffic scenes. Comprehensive evaluations on the nuScenes
and Argoverse 2 datasets demonstrate that CAA-GMM achieves
competitive or superior accuracy compared with state-of-the-
art raster-based baselines, while maintaining low computational
complexity. Ablation analyses confirm the importance of the
attention module for robust contextual reasoning and predictive
precision. Furthermore, evaluations under imperfect commu-
nication and perception conditions highlight the framework’s
resilience to uncertainty, establishing CAA-GMM as an efficient
and scalable solution for cooperative trajectory prediction in
intelligent transportation systems.

I. INTRODUCTION
Accurate trajectory prediction is a cornerstone of au-
tonomous and connected driving, enabling safe planning and
cooperative decision-making in dynamic traffic environments.
Vehicles must anticipate the future motions of surround-
ing agents—including vehicles, cyclists, and pedestrians—to
safely navigate complex scenarios such as intersections,
merges, and dense urban traffic [1], [2]. This task is inherently
challenging due to the multimodal nature of human behavior,
sensor noise, occlusions, and uncertainty arising from partial
observability and interaction dynamics [3]–[6].

Recent deep learning approaches for trajectory prediction
are commonly categorized into raster-based, graph-based,
and planning-aware paradigms. Raster-based methods en-
code agents and map context as BEV images processed by
convolutional or transformer architectures [7]–[9], with re-
cent attention-based variants such as CASPFormer improving
contextual reasoning while remaining computationally effi-
cient [10]
Graph-based approaches explicitly model agents and map
elements as nodes connected via relational edges, capturing
interactions through message passing or self-attention [11]–
[15]. These methods excel at interaction modeling but of-
ten require high-definition maps and complex preprocessing
pipelines, limiting scalability and robustness in partially ob-
served or communication-constrained settings. Planning-aware
models further integrate intent or goal inference to couple
prediction and decision-making [16]–[18]. Although effective
in structured autonomous scenarios, their reliance on rational
intent assumptions and explicit goal annotations can reduce
performance in mixed or human-driven traffic.
In parallel, cooperative perception and prediction have
gained increasing attention with the emergence of Vehicle-
to-Everything (V2X) communication. Model-Based Commu-
nication (MBC) frameworks propose exchanging compact
probabilistic representations—rather than dense trajectories
or raw sensor data—to reduce bandwidth while preserving
predictive fidelity [19]–[22]. However, existing learning-based
predictors are not explicitly designed to operate under realistic
communication constraints, nor do they jointly account for
communication loss and perception degradation.
To address these limitations, this paper proposes the
Context-Aware Attention-based Gaussian Mixture Model
(CAA-GMM), a lightweight probabilistic trajectory predic-
tion framework tailored for cooperative driving. CAA-GMM
operates within the MBC paradigm by transmitting Gaussian
mixture parameters, achieving communication efficiency while
remaining compatible with SAE J2735 Basic Safety Message
constraints [23]. The model fuses rasterized static-map features
with learned embeddings of agent motion histories and neigh-
boring interactions using a transformer-based attention mecha-
nism. A Gaussian mixture decoder then produces interpretable
multimodal trajectory distributions with explicit uncertainty

Comprehensive evaluations on nuScenes [24] and Argov-

II. CONTEXT-AWARE ATTENTION-BASED GAUSSIAN
MIXTURE MODELS
This section presents the proposed Context-Aware Attention-
based Gaussian Mixture Model (CAA-GMM) for vehicular
trajectory prediction. We first briefly introduce a reduced
variant, the Context-Aware Gaussian Mixture Model (CA-
GMM), which provides the underlying Bayesian formulation
for multimodal inference. CA-GMM is described only to
establish notation, mixture-based conditioning, regression, and
mode construction. We then present CAA-GMM, our main
contribution, which extends CA-GMM with mode-level and
interaction-level attention to enable socially consistent and
context-sensitive prediction.

B. Context-Aware Attention-based Gaussian Mixture Model
(CAA-GMM)
Accurate multi-agent trajectory prediction requires model-
ing both contextual cues and inter-agent dependencies. While
the Context-Aware Gaussian Mixture Model (CA-GMM) pro-
vides a strong probabilistic baseline by combining Bayesian
inference with contextual classification, it models each agent
independently and lacks explicit interaction reasoning. To
overcome this limitation, we propose the Context-Aware
Attention-based Gaussian Mixture Model (CAA-GMM), an
extension of CA-GMM that incorporates transformer-based at-
tention mechanisms to model dynamic, type-aware interactions
between agents. By combining raster-based visual context with
structured agent states (positions, velocities, and headings),
CAA-GMM learns to weigh the influence of neighboring
agents on the ego agent’s future motion, improving both
accuracy and social consistency.
Inspired by recent lightweight transformer architectures for
trajectory forecasting [26]–[30], our model removes absolute
and relative positional embeddings to enhance generalization
across diverse layouts. In addition, we introduce a type-aware
attention masking mechanism that modulates attention scores
based on agent-type pairs (e.g., vehicle-to-vehicle, vehicle-to-
pedestrian). This design allows the model to learn distinct
interaction patterns between heterogeneous agents without
requiring explicit type embeddings, resulting in a scalable and
semantically structured formulation.
1) System Architecture Overview: From a system-level per-
spective, CAA-GMM maintains the multi-modal probabilistic
foundation of CA-GMM while introducing two additional
transformer modules: (i) a mode-level encoder and (ii) a type-
aware interaction decoder. Together, these modules enable
both inter-modal reasoning (relationships among Gaussian tra-
jectory modes) and inter-agent reasoning (social dependencies
between agents).
Mode-Level Transformer Encoder.: The mode-level en-
coder captures dependencies across the K Gaussian trajectory
modes that would otherwise be treated independently. Each
mode is represented as a token in the transformer sequence.
At each encoder layer, the model applies self-attention among
mode tokens to capture inter-modal dependencies (e.g., mu-
tual exclusivity between turning and going straight), followed
by cross-attention between mode tokens and image em-
beddings extracted from the BEV encoder. This design allows
each mode to jointly reason about other plausible futures while
grounding its representation in spatial scene context. Con-
sistent with efficient transformer design [26]–[28], positional

2) Feature Extractors and Embedding Blocks: Feature ex-
traction from the rasterized BEV context is performed using a
modified ResNet-34 backbone. The network is initialized with
ImageNet-1k pretrained weights, employs an output stride of
8 via dilated convolutions, and integrates a feature pyramid
network (FPN) for multi-scale fusion. The initial 7×7 convo-
lutional stem is replaced with stacked 3×3 layers for improved
spatial resolution, and normalization layers are frozen during
warm-up to stabilize training.
The high-dimensional BEV features are processed by an
image embedder block (Linear–ReLU–Linear). In parallel,
the ego embedder encodes the concatenation of the ego state
history and the posterior parameters (posterior mean for speed
and heading and BLS estimation for position) of each Gaussian
mode through a single linear layer. The output dimensions of
both image and ego embeddings are matched so that they can
be concatenated and fed into the mode-level encoder.
To represent other agents, a neighbor embedder projects
their kinematic features (position, velocity, heading) via a
single linear layer with an output size twice that of the
ego embedding, allowing for richer representation of social
interactions. Both transformer encoder and decoder blocks fol-
low standard practice, comprising multi-head attention, layer
normalization with dropout, and feedforward sublayers.
3) Integration with the Bayesian Decoder and Training:
The interaction-aware mode representations are first scored by
the classifier to produce logits over the K Gaussian modes.
The top-K modes with the highest classifier confidence are
selected, and their corresponding latent representations are
forwarded to a lightweight residual regressor.
The residual regressor is implemented as a simple MLP-
Mixer–style module that mixes information across mode chan-
nels and temporal dimensions. Given the selected mode
embeddings, it predicts a residual trajectory vector of size
Tfut × 2 (corresponding to (∆x, ∆y)) for each selected mode.
These residuals refine the Bayesian trajectory estimates pro-
duced by the GMM decoder, compensating for interaction-
induced deviations that are not captured by the base Gaussian
regression.
Final position trajectories are obtained by combining the
Bayesian BLS estimation with the predicted residual offsets.
CAA-GMM is trained end-to-end using the same multi-term
objective (12) as CA-GMM, enabling consistent ablation and
fair comparison.

III. ANALYSIS AND RESULTS
We evaluate the proposed models on the nuScenes [24]
and Argoverse 2 [25] motion forecasting benchmarks. Both
datasets provide large-scale multi-agent trajectories with HD
map context across diverse urban environments. A unified pre-
processing pipeline is applied to generate ego-centric bird’s-
eye-view (BEV) raster representations aligned with the ego
heading.
A. Datasets
nuScenes contains 1,000 urban driving scenes. Following
the standard protocol, we predict 6 s of future motion given
2 s of history, yielding over 34,000 annotated trajectories.
Rasterized BEV inputs encode static map elements together
with dynamic agents.
Argoverse 2 (AV2) provides large-scale multi-agent trajec-
tories with complex lane topology. Models are trained using
5 s of history for benchmark evaluation and 3 s for ablation
studies under communication and observation loss.

Performance is evaluated using standard trajectory predic-
tion metrics, including minimum Average Displacement Error
(minADE), minimum Final Displacement Error (minFDE),
and Miss Rate (MR), computed over the top-K predicted
trajectories.

Tables I and II report quantitative results on nuScenes and
Argoverse 2 with a 6 s prediction horizon. On nuScenes, CA-
GMM consistently outperforms raster-based baselines such
as MTP and CoverNet, demonstrating the benefit of explicit
Gaussian-based probabilistic inference for modeling multi-
modal futures.
The proposed CAA-GMM further improves performance by
incorporating attention-based interaction modeling, achieving
competitive or superior accuracy compared to recent attention-
based raster models such as CASPFormer and AgentFormer.
Notably, CAA-GMM approaches the performance of advanced
graph-based methods (e.g., SemanticFormerR and LAformer)
without requiring explicit relational graph construction or
costly preprocessing. On Argoverse 2, CAA-GMM exhibits
strong generalization across complex traffic scenarios and
long-horizon prediction, achieving performance comparable
to high-capacity graph- and query-based approaches such as
TrajAngleNet, QML, and BANet. These results indicate that
combining probabilistic Gaussian inference with lightweight
attention mechanisms yields a favorable balance between


predictive accuracy, computational efficiency, and scalability.
Finally, ablation results reveal a trade-off between the
number of Gaussian components and generalization. While
increasing the number of modes improves representational
capacity, excessively large mixtures can degrade performance
due to overfitting and mode fragmentation. This highlights the
importance of selecting an appropriate number of Gaussian
modes to balance prediction diversity and robustness.

the most recent available states and forecasts its trajectory over
(5, 11] s.
